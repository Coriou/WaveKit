# Signal discovery scanner

The scanner finds and characterises RF activity on a source, confirms it with a real decode where a decoder exists, records it, and reports each distinct emitter once as a _discovery_ with evidence. In the web app it is the **Scanner** drawer ([web app guide](WEB-APP.md#drawers)): watch the band in view or plan a scan, see the signals found, skip the ones you do not care about. The CLI's `6 Scan` view is a second client. The scanner runs inside the core and is driven over REST/WS (`/api/scanner`, channel `scanner`); its activity layer is the core spectrum service (`/api/spectrum`, channel `spectrum`), which also feeds the web and CLI waterfalls.

Design: [scanner spec](superpowers/specs/2026-10-09-signal-discovery-scanner-design.md). Endpoint and message reference: [API.md, Scanner and Spectrum](API.md#scanner). Component view: [ARCHITECTURE.md, Scanner](ARCHITECTURE.md#scanner).

While watching a source, the scanner supplies its current-epoch, gated energy centres
as extra dsd-fme activity candidates. Centres are rounded to the nearest 6.25 kHz
raster (initial ties upward). Each track keeps its previous raster centre until its
actual centre moves more than 500 Hz past the midpoint: distance > 3125 + 500 =
3625 Hz. This gives a 1000 Hz return deadband and at most 3625 Hz identity error.
The same stable centre is returned to the planner and used for coalesced notices;
back-and-forth midpoint jitter does not
force a planner pass. Only the current candidate set per epoch and decoder is retained. This allows digital voice on an unlisted channel
to receive a decoder without a protocol hint or VFO selection. Suggestions remain
subject to decoder band, passband fit, source ownership and CPU limits; energy alone
does not verify a protocol. Checked dsd-fme output must pass the existing verification
and receiver provenance rules. See [decoder activity placement](DECODER-GUIDE.md).

## What it does and does not do

- Finds energy in the window a source is tuned to (in-window) or across wider ranges by retuning an rtl_tcp source (sweep).
- Classifies emissions from their spectrum (bandwidth, shape, TDMA envelope) and confirms them with dsd-fme, multimon-ng or direwolf.
- Never decrypts and never handles keys. Encrypted traffic is reported as `encrypted: true` and no audio is kept.
- Confidence is honest: `activity` (energy only) → `candidate` (spectral hypothesis) → `classified` (channel features; after the channelizer) → `decoded` (the confirm rule of the protocol was met: for DMR, the same colour code on two content-verified bursts or checked voice link-control lines). `decoded` means the first **accepted** identity, not any identity-bearing evidence.
- Before the channelizer: one confirm probe at a time (each costs about half a core), analog replay characterisation when IQ capture is enabled, no listen-scan, ISM only through configured rtl_433 decoders (ambient evidence).

## Modes

### In-window

The job watches the current window of the source and never sends tuner commands. If an operator or an SDR++ relay client retunes, the job follows the new window: open observations end with `retune` and detection resumes after the settle time. Operator (non-scanner) retunes use their own 150 ms display settle and stale-content cap; spectrum frames published before `scanner.sweep.settleMs.network` has elapsed after such a retune are labelled `unverified`. The scanner's own hops keep their settle; a hop also commands the sample rate (the job's, else the declared one) when the rate is only declared or was not written on the current connection, so its epoch is `commanded` under the strict trust rule.

### Sweep

The job takes the source's tuner lease and steps through a hop plan (ascending centres, about 80 % of the sample rate each, overlapping by the widest channel). On each hop it settles (test-mode markers, or a timer when markers are not available), dwells, and either moves on or holds to confirm a track. Revisits favour busy hops: after every `round(1 / revisit.share)` coarse hops (share 0.3), the hop with the highest activity × time since the last visit is inserted again.

**Fair holds.** Every frequency is held like any other signal until a hold shows that it is a carrier: a confirm probe on each visit, and analog voice (`analog-fm`, `analog-am`, with `hold.policy: "hold"`) followed while it is on air. Only a frequency with positive carrier evidence is held under the carrier rules below.

- A visit's holds normally last at most one nominal coarse sweep (the sum of the hop dwells, at most `hold.maxHoldMs`): a confirm hold ends there, further confirm holds are chained only within it, and a follow is cut there unless the rules below let it run longer. The tracks left over are held on the hop's next visit. So that a short sweep can still confirm, a confirm hold may always run its class window up to 12 s plus the probe start latency (the longest built-in class hold, 10 s, with 2 s of decoder start), never past `hold.maxHoldMs`. A longer `confirmHoldMs` is cut to that bound, and the signal is probed again on the hop's later visits.
- The scheduler remembers per frequency what its holds heard. Overlapping hops share it, and it is kept across re-plans and while a preempted job waits (a core restart starts from nothing). It is forgotten after ten full visits in a row that could have heard the frequency and did not, evidence and retry budget together (a resting carrier too); time spent holding another hop or a source outage forgets nothing. A gap that a visit heard counts: a close and a reopening, a close and the end of the visit, a frequency that opens after the visit listened for it, or a visit that listened without hearing it at all. A gap of 1 s or more is a turnaround: the frequency is **traffic**. A close and reopening less than 1 s apart is a flap, which is carrier evidence. A gap no visit heard (it went off while the hop was away) proves nothing. A visit restarted by an outage, a preemption or a resume heard nothing in between, so a close before the restart is no heard gap.
- **Traffic** (a heard gap of 1 s or more, or a decode) is held on every visit. Voice on it is followed for at most L from the start of the visit's holds, so that a QSO on one hop cannot hide a 28 s ID on another: L = max(one coarse sweep, 28 s − one sweep away − 5 s), where one sweep away is (hops − 1 + ⌈hops / 3⌉) × hop time + settle + (`minSurfaceBlocks` + 1) blocks. With 1.15 s hops (1 s settle, 50 ms blocks) L is 19.55 s on 2 hops, 18.4 s on 3 hops and 11.5 s on 7 hops; on a plan whose hops are longer, L is one coarse sweep. These follows, carrier holds and recording holds share L per hop: between two visits of any hop, the other hops' follows, carrier holds and recording holds last at most L in all (an 8 s watch is not cut, but counts; a carrier hold's 1 s hang to class a gap ends within L too, and a visit restarted by an outage, a preemption or a resume keeps what its holds already spent; the time a job was paused is not counted, while a source outage is, because the job keeps ticking through it, so the other hops wait less, never more), so a busy hop followed on its coarse visit and again on a revisit gets L in all. A follow that ends sooner (the over ended) leaves the rest of L to the next one, and its hop is followed again on its next visit. Once a hop has waited L for them (or after a watch, a recording hold or a decoded follow longer than one coarse sweep), every hop gets a coarse visit before the next long follow, with no carrier hold in that sweep, and voice on the hop that spent it is only probed until then. A 28 s call on a frequency already known as traffic (a second ID on the same channel) is therefore heard for at most L in one hold, and its end can be missed (measured on 3 hops: 18.15 and 20.8 s of 28 s, where a follow to `hold.maxHoldMs` heard 27.05 s). A call a decoder confirms during its probe is followed to `hold.maxHoldMs` (not cut to L); only a decoded follow that reaches L sets the lock, so a channel of 10 s decoded calls is followed call after call. A decode without a follow (policy `confirm`, voice not recorded) ends the probe: the frequency is probed again on later visits, so a new call after a turnaround is identified too.
- **A frequency not known yet** is held on every visit, and each hold watches it for at most 8 s, after its probe if it has one, never `hold.maxHoldMs`. A gap heard during the hold settles it either way. If it was already on air when its hop was first visited (at the job start, for example), on air without a break for those 8 s is carrier evidence. So a new carrier keeps the other hops waiting for 8 s once (plus up to 1.05 s to class a gap). Five carriers on air when a 60-hop plan of 1.15 s hops starts lengthen its first sweep from 69 s by at most 5 × 9.05 s, to about 114 s plus revisits (measured 109 s).
- **A call whose start the sweep saw** (a full visit of its hop missed it before it appeared) may be longer than 8 s, so 8 s on air is no carrier evidence for it. After its first 8 s watch, every hop gets a coarse visit (with no carrier hold in that sweep), then analog voice is followed on until it has been on air for 30 s since it was first heard; the sweep away in between is not heard. Later holds watch it for 8 s again, each after a coarse visit of every hop. On air for `hold.maxHoldMs` with no close heard and no full visit missing it, it is a carrier. So a carrier that switches on mid-job keeps the other hops waiting for 8 s, then at most 30 s of its run less a sweep, then 8 s per sweep, and a 28 s ID on another hop is still detected. Two such calls at once share the time: on a 7-hop plan the second may end while the sweep is away (detected 14.4 s after its start and heard 8.2 s of 28 s, against 4.5 s by one confirm probe per visit).
- **What a 28 s ID gets.** With one tuner, two calls on different hops cannot both be heard to their end, and detecting every call comes first. This holds for the plans it is tested on: 2 to 7 hops of 1.15 s with bounded competing activity (at most one carrier, one busy channel and one other call). On a plan whose coarse sweep is longer than the ID (a 60-hop plan takes 69 s without holds), a 28 s ID can start and end between two visits of its hop. On the tested plans, a 28 s ID that starts after the job's first sweeps is detected while it is on air, except rarely when a QSO and a second call are both on air on other hops (about 0.1 %, below). On a plan without revisits it is heard at least as long as one confirm probe per visit (the dwell and the confirm hold on each visit of its hop) would hear it. With revisits, that scan can visit the hop more often, and an ID detected late can be heard a little less (measured: up to 0.7 s less, on a 7-hop plan, for an ID detected more than 12 s after its start). It is heard to its end when two things hold: no other call whose start the sweep saw is on air at any time during it, and it is detected at least 8 s plus one sweep away plus two blocks before its end. One sweep away is every other hop's dwell, one revisit per three coarse hops and the settle when its hop is tuned again: (hops − 1 + ⌈hops / 3⌉) × hop time + settle. With 1.15 s hops (1 s settle) on 3 hops that is 4.45 s, so the ID must be detected within 15.45 s of its start; on 7 hops, 11.35 s and 8.55 s. An ID that is detected later (its hop was just left, behind other hops' holds), or that shares the air with another such call, may end while the sweep is away: on a 7-hop plan, an ID detected 12.35 s after its start is heard 8.15 s, and its hop is back 300 ms after its end.
- **A carrier** has positive carrier evidence: a hold heard it on air without a break for 8 s (for `hold.maxHoldMs` if it was traffic or a call whose start the sweep saw, with no decode), or heard it flap. After each hold it rests for twice that hold's length on average (from 1 to 3 times its length, spread so retries do not lock onto regular overs). A frequency that was traffic before, or whose start the sweep saw, rests once its hold's length instead (a repeater whose tail bridges its turnarounds is on air for minutes without a break, then gives an ID). A flapping carrier rests twenty times its hold. An emitter never known as a call (on air since before any visit saw it start, no turnaround heard) earns that from its first flap; a frequency known as a call earns it only with a second flap heard soon after the first, with no turnaround heard in between: within 2 × min(one coarse sweep, 8 s) + 1 s + 6 blocks + the dwell of listening, counting the visit that hears it from its tune and the earlier visits of its hops while they held (with 1.15 s hops and 50 ms blocks, 9.35 s on 3 hops and 18.45 s on 7 hops). A carrier that flaps faster than its holds last flaps in every hold, and two flaps of consecutive holds are less than that apart, so it earns the rest from its second flap; one quick turnaround does not make a QSO rest twenty times longer. At most one carrier hold runs per coarse sweep, and each lasts at most one coarse sweep, at most 8 s and at most what L has left (it is skipped when nothing is left). A carrier that never flapped but that a full visit missed (it went off air, which a steady carrier never does) no longer rests: it is held in every sweep's carrier slot. And a dwell that misses such a carrier while its retry is due listens on, at most 1 s more, for the gap: still off after 1 s, it is traffic. So a steady carrier takes about a third of the time (at most half), a known call about half (at most two thirds) and a flapping one at most an eleventh; on a 60-hop plan of 1.15 s hops a sweep with a carrier hold takes 77 s instead of 69 s. A heard gap of 1 s or more, or a decode, makes it traffic again. A carrier that was already on air when its hop was first visited counts as no activity, so its hop gets no revisits for it.
- Why a carrier still costs a third of the time: a QSO already on air when the job starts can show carrier evidence (an over heard for 8 s without a break). It becomes traffic again only when a hold hears one of its turnarounds. That happens at a rate of about the carrier's share of the time divided by the over period, so 45 s overs are found as traffic after about 138 s on average. A carrier share of a sixth would double that and hear such a QSO less than one confirm probe per visit did. Retries also tend to start just after a turnaround (a retry due while the frequency is off waits for the next over), which is why a missed visit makes a carrier be held every sweep and a due retry that finds it off listens for the gap: a QSO of regular overs on any hop or hop overlap of 3- and 7-hop plans is then heard at least as much as one confirm probe per visit heard it (at least 18 % more in the measured 45, 35, 25 and 20 s overs).
- Analog voice is followed while it is on air (after the digital-voice check probe, or at once when no confirmation is needed), then for `hangMs`. A new call on the same frequency during the hang is followed on, within the same bound. On a frequency not known as traffic the hang lasts at least 1.05 s, whatever `hangMs` is, so the gap is classed; it may run that far past the hold's bound. A track that closes during its probe is held in its hang too. A track too young to be classified when the dwell ends (4 blocks or fewer read as a burst) whose spectrum reads as analog voice (an FM voice width, or an AM voice width with a narrow or peaked shape) makes the dwell wait once for its fifth block, so a call labelled late can still be followed; any other young burst leaves at the dwell. Signals-only jobs do not hold.

- **Quick turnarounds: the accepted exception.** A QSO whose turnarounds are under 1 s cannot be told from a carrier that flaps (a 0.5 s gap reads like the 0.3 s gaps of a carrier on air 20 s at a time), so it is held as a carrier and heard less than one confirm probe per visit heard it. Measured over 900 s with four seeds, a QSO on hop 0 beside 28 s IDs on the last hop: with 0.3 to 0.9 s turnarounds 371 s of air heard against 1659 s on 3 hops, and 300 s against 1018 s on 7 hops; with 0.5 to 1.5 s turnarounds 805 s against 1612 s and 540 s against 980 s. The same holds for a QSO that starts mid-job: from 60 s with 0.3 to 0.9 s turnarounds, 430 s against 1219 s on 3 hops (about a third) and 442 s against 912 s on 7 hops (about half). Every ID is still detected. A QSO with turnarounds of 1 s or more is heard more than one confirm probe per visit heard it (1.4 to 1.6 times on those scenes), with one known exception: on 3 hops, a QSO on air from the job start can be heard a little less, as with seed 22 of that scene (354 s against 370 s, 4.4 % less).
- **Recording.** A sweep waits for the scanner's analog voice recorder (see [Analog voice recordings](#analog-voice-recordings)): while the recorder reports a transmission being recorded in the window of the hop the sweep is tuned to, the sweep keeps the hop instead of leaving it when its holds are done: up to what L has left from the start of the visit's holds (never past `hold.maxHoldMs`), re-checked every 250 ms, and left at the first re-check that finds the recording ended. **The exception: record whole voice** (the chosen policy). While voice is on air in the recorded transmission (classed `analog-fm`, passed the voice gate, and voice heard in that transmission within the last 2 s of the audio the recorder has judged), the hop is kept until that transmission ends, up to `hold.maxHoldMs` from the start of the visit's holds (60 s by default) whatever L has left, so a 28 s ID is recorded whole. The voice hold ends when that transmission's squelch closes, even when the recorder's `hangMs` joins the next over into the same file, and whatever hold runs meanwhile (a probe or follow of another signal in the window): the recorder names the voiced transmission (its track) with each voice answer, the sweep remembers that name when the voice hold starts and asks the recorder every 250 ms while the hold is on (and at every track close outside the recording hold), and the voice hold ends at the first answer that is not voice or names another transmission. How long the recorder went unasked never ends it, so a stalled event loop does not cut a held over short, and a turnaround hidden inside such a stall still ends the hold once the next over is named. On simplex a turnaround closes the squelch, so the hold never runs across a QSO turnaround. Through a repeater it can: the repeater's hang carrier keeps the over's track open across a turnaround shorter than the hang, and voice counts as on air until about 3.25 s after the last word, so a turnaround under about 1.75 s is not seen and the hold runs across overs up to `hold.maxHoldMs`. Measured in simulation on the scene below with the QSO through a repeater (a 1.5 or 3 s hang, the sweep seeing a track per over), about 35 to 42 of 152 IDs on the other hop are missed on 3 hops and about 20 to 35 on 7 hops, against 15 and 4 on simplex. Later voice in the same visit keeps only what L has left, and so does every later hold of the visit. Then every hop gets a full coarse visit before the hop is followed or kept again, so the next over of the same QSO gets a voice hold only after that sweep. A carrier or a repeater hang after the voice, or a stuck carrier, keeps the hop at most L: once no voice has been heard for 2 s (about 3.5 s after the last word, because the recorder judges audio 1 s behind the air), the hold falls back to what L has left, and is left at once if L is already spent. A shadow recording (not classed yet) and a recording not voiced yet never keep the hop. **Its cost, measured** in simulation on the scene of a QSO (overs of 5 to 30 s, 1 to 3 s turnarounds) on hop 0 and 28 s IDs on the last hop, 900 s, four seeds, QSO from the job start and from 60 s (152 IDs per plan): 15 of 152 IDs on the other hop are missed on 3 hops and 4 of 152 on 7 hops, against none without the voice hold. In exchange the QSO is heard 489 s per run instead of 425 s on 3 hops, and 358 s instead of 307 s on 7 hops. A lone 28 s ID is kept to its end: of 41 start times from 20 to 40 s, 38 are heard for at least 25 s on 3 hops and 19 on 7 hops (the rest were detected late), where L alone heard none that long. A new signal in the window is still probed or followed meanwhile, within that same L: every later hold of the visit ends by then, and new traffic evidence does not shorten it. This counts as a long follow (it spends L and sets the lock), it is skipped while the lock holds, and a hop is never kept for it on a parked one-hop job, a signals-only job or under `hold.policy: "never"`. Nor is a hop kept when the recording can only be of frequencies with carrier evidence (the recorder names the window, not the frequency, so every analog voice on air in the window counts; one without carrier evidence keeps the hop and is the one reported): they stay under the carrier rules, so a carrier labelled analog voice is not held on every visit again (measured on 3 hops with a recorder that records analog voice on air plus a 2 s tail: a labelled carrier's hop takes 34 % of the time and every ID on another hop is heard to its end, a carrier flapping 3 s on / 0.5 s off 47 %; kept for its recording, 46 % with 2 of 3 ID ends missed, and 70 %). The `hold.maxHoldMs` bound and an abandoned confirm still leave at once.

So between two visits of a hop the scheduler spends at most one coarse sweep of dwells and its revisits, and holds of one coarse sweep each (plus up to 1.05 s to class a gap, and one confirm window of at most 12 s and the probe start where a confirm needs it). On top of that come at most one carrier hold (or a dwell listening 1 s more for a carrier's gap), an 8 s watch for each frequency not known, and once per call whose start the sweep saw, its follow to 30 s of its run. Follows of voice on traffic, carrier holds and recording holds share L between two visits of any hop, and once a hop has waited L every hop gets a coarse visit. Decoded follows and holds for a voice recording are the exception: one costs up to `hold.maxHoldMs` (a decoded follow sets the lock once it reaches L or outlasts one coarse sweep; a voice hold sets it whenever it ends). While a follow runs, the other hops are not visited at all. The scheduler does not peek at other hops during a follow: a peek costs a retune and a settle (about 1 s with a timer settle) and would cut the followed call.

What is shown, and on what: in simulation of 2 to 7 hops of 1.15 s (one carrier, one busy channel, one other call at most), every 28 s ID that starts after the job's first sweeps is detected while on air beside a QSO with real 1 to 3 s turnarounds on another hop (measured: detected at most 24.7 s after its start, on 3 hops), as long as the recorder keeps no hop for voice. With the analog voice recorder on (the default), an over held to its end can hide an ID on another hop: 15 of 152 missed on 3 hops and 4 of 152 on 7 hops on that scene (see Recording above). With a second call also on air (8 to 20 s calls on a third hop), about 0.1 % of IDs can be missed: 2 of 1635 over 900 s on 3, 5 and 7 hops with 40 seeds each, where one confirm probe per visit missed none. This is an accepted current limit: a hop whose follows spent L is still revisited for probes, and those probe holds are not counted against L, so an ID can start and end while its hop waits. Beside such a QSO, the time an ID is heard is not bounded by one confirm probe per visit (the QSO's follows take that time; measured from 2.25 s on 3 hops), and its end is not guaranteed. A decoded call on another hop can still hide a 28 s ID for up to `hold.maxHoldMs`. Nor is a 28 s call guaranteed during a job's first sweeps, while the 8 s watches run: the first sweep costs about 9.05 s more per frequency not known on air at the job start, linearly (60 such carriers, one per hop of a 60-hop plan: 549 s instead of 69 s; later sweeps 77 s).

**Gain per band.** A hop with no job gain (`tuner.gainTenthsDb`) takes the gain of the per-band gain table entry holding its centre (`tuner.bandGain`; by default 37.2 dB in the 868 and 915 MHz ISM bands, your own gain elsewhere). A later hop outside the bands, the lease release, and crash recovery give your exact gain mode and gain back; an AGC gain mode is restored after the gain, so the tuner ends in AGC. When your own write (a retune, a sample rate) takes the tuner from the job, only your gain mode and gain go back, at once, unless that write set the gain, gain mode or tuner gain index; after a relay client, they go back once control returns, unless the relay client set a gain, gain mode or tuner gain index. While your gain or gain mode is unknown (right after a restart, before any gain write), hops write no band gain. Manual tuning, including a dwell you drive through the tuner API, never applies the table unless `tuner.bandGain.applyToMyTuning` is on. See [CORE-REFERENCE.md, Per-band gain](CORE-REFERENCE.md#per-band-gain).

Worked example: 400–470 MHz at 2.048 Msps is 44 hops. With markers (about 30 ms counter run + 5 ms PLL settle) and 50 ms blocks, a hop dwells about 185 ms, so a coarse sweep takes about 8 s (about 11.6 s when every revisit slot is used). With a 1 s timer settle (markers do not pass, typically over Wi-Fi) a hop costs about 1.15 s and a sweep about 51 s. These are assumptions; `POST /api/scanner/plan` reports the real numbers for your source (`sweepPeriodMs`, measured settle once known).

**Preview period and probability of intercept.** The preview runs the sweep scheduler itself on the plan, on an empty band, with the source's recent occupancy as the revisit seed (the seed a new job starts with): `sweepPeriodMs` is the simulated period (dwells end on whole detection blocks; revisits only where the band was recently active), and a hop's detection POI is the chance that a `txDurationHintMs` transmission starting at a random time is on air for `minSurfaceBlocks` blocks (`N`) inside one of that hop's simulated listening spans, about `(D + d − 2NT) / P` for one visit of `d` listening per period `P`, and 0 when `D` or `d` is shorter than `NT`. Holds on signals come on top of the simulated period. Decode POI also needs the transmission to stay on air for the confirm hold after the dwell ends: `(D − NT − confirm) / P` per visit, 0 when `D < NT + confirm`. The simulation is bounded: three sweeps after a warm-up sweep, taken as one repeating period (so a long `txDurationHintMs` costs nothing more), quiet blocks skipped in one step, and plans of more than 512 hops use the plain round-robin period (settle plus dwell per hop, no revisits) instead.

### Passive job

One per eligible source (`passive-<sourceId>`): in-window, all protocols, lowest priority. It is on by default and can be disabled per source at runtime. Before the channelizer it confirms only through ambient evidence, unless `scanner.passive.offsetProbes: true`. On hosts where passive scanning costs more than 25 % of a core (a Pi 4), passive switches itself off and the status says why (`engines[].passive.reasonCode` as a closed code, `disabledReason` with the figure). The limit is `scanner.passive.maxEngineUtilization` (config only, 0.05 to 1, absent = 0.25). The guard first judges 60 s after the engine opened, then every 5 min, each time over the window since its previous check (not the engine's whole life).

- **Passive alone on the engine** (a headless receiver): the whole engine cost is judged against the limit (`engine-cpu N % of a core`).
- **Engine shared with the waterfall or another job**: only what passive adds is judged. The guard pauses passive for one minute to measure the engine without it (status `measuring baseline`, at most once per 30 min), then judges the difference against the limit (`passive-cpu N % of a core`). A shared engine at 95 % of a core or more is saturated and passive is switched off at once (`engine-cpu`). The baseline is measured again when the waterfall or another job joins or leaves, the engine's worker restarts, or it is 30 min old.
- **Coming back**: a disabled source whose engine (without passive) runs at or below 80 % of the limit comes back; with the waterfall still open, passive is tried again every 15 min for one minute and stays on if its own cost is within the limit. A disabled source whose engine closed is put on probation after 15 min: passive is re-enabled and the next engine open is judged again.

## Jobs

`POST /api/scanner/jobs` takes a `ScanJobSpec`. Every field is optional; the normalised spec (`job.spec`) carries a `provenance` per field: `operator` (set in the spec), `bandplan`, `measured`, `config` (an explicit setting, today only `fftSize`) or `default`.

| Field              | Default                                                  | Meaning                                                                                                                                    |
| ------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `name`             | none                                                     | Label                                                                                                                                      |
| `sourceId`         | the default source                                       | Source to scan                                                                                                                             |
| `mode`             | `auto`                                                   | `in-window`, `sweep`, or `auto` (in-window when the scope fits the current window)                                                         |
| `kind`             | `discover`                                               | `monitor` (listen-scan) needs the channelizer; `identify` only via `/identify`                                                             |
| `ranges`           | the current window                                       | `[{startHz, endHz, rasters?, obwHz?, protocols?, enabled?, name?, dwellMs?, sensitivity?}]`, see [Per-range settings](#per-range-settings) |
| `bandplans`        | none                                                     | Built-in bandplan ids, expanded into ranges                                                                                                |
| `exclusions`       | none                                                     | `[{startHz, endHz, reason?}]`                                                                                                              |
| `protocols`        | covering ranges per frequency, else all                  | Priors for classification and the protocols to confirm                                                                                     |
| `sensitivity`      | `scanner.detection.sensitivity` (`normal`)               | Preset, see [Presets and bandplans](#presets-and-bandplans)                                                                                |

| `tuner`            | unchanged                                                | Sweep only: `sampleRateHz`, `gainTenthsDb`, `agc`, `overload: flag \| reduce-gain`                                                         |
| `stop`             | none                                                     | `afterSweeps`, `afterMs` (≥ 1000), `onFirstDecoded`                                                                                        |
| `priority`         | 50                                                       | 0–100, among scanner jobs only                                                                                                             |
| `takeover`         | `false`                                                  | Consent to disturb other consumers (sweep)                                                                                                 |
| `resume`           | `{autoAfterIdleMs: 60000, onBoot: false}`                | Auto-resume after preemption; resume after a core restart                                                                                  |

| `capture`          | `{enabled: false}`                                       | Burst IQ capture, see [Burst IQ capture](#burst-iq-capture-fixtures)                                                                       |
| `signalsOnly`      | `false`                                                  | Discover jobs: find signals, never start decoders, see [Signals only](#signals-only)                                                       |

The API converts two § 5.1 shorthands before planning: a range's `spacingHz` with optional `firstChannelHz` (default `startHz + spacingHz / 2`) becomes one raster, and an exclusion `{frequencyHz, widthHz}` becomes `{startHz, endHz}` centred on the frequency.

### Per-range settings

Each range can carry its own settings; a range without them behaves as before.

- `enabled: false` keeps the range in the job (same position, same fields) but skips it: it is not swept, masked or used to pick protocols. A job needs at least one enabled range (`range-empty` otherwise).
- `name` labels the range (1 to 100 characters).
- `dwellMs` lengthens or shortens the dwell of the hops that overlap the range. A hop that overlaps several enabled ranges uses the longest of their dwells (a range without one counts at the job's `dwellMs`); the plan shows it as `hop.dwellMs`, and the sweep period adds up the hop dwells. The preview's probability of intercept (detection and decode) for such a hop uses its own dwell; hops without one use the job's `dwellMs`.
- `sensitivity` (`low`, `normal`, `high`) sets the detector's false-alarm target inside the range. Integration time stays job-wide, set by the job's `sensitivity`.

### Editing a running job's ranges

`PATCH /api/scanner/jobs/:id` with `ranges` edits a job while it runs. Send back the job's whole `spec.ranges` list (operator ranges first, then bandplan ranges) with the changes; the count and order must match. You can switch ranges on and off and change their `protocols`, `name`, `dwellMs` and `sensitivity`; leaving one of these out of a range clears it. Edges, channel spacing (`rasters`), `obwHz` and the bandplan a range came from are fixed while the job exists: changing them answers 400 `SCANNER_PLAN_INVALID` with a `range-locked` issue naming the range and the fields. Stop the job and create a new one to change them. The hop plan changes at the next hop; masks, protocol matching and probes follow as soon as the PATCH returns. Edits are saved with the job, survive a restart, and are kept by later patches of other keys. If the job's band plan changes under it, an edit applies only to the range it was made for (same position and edges); edits that no longer match are dropped once, with a `range-locked` warning in that PATCH's answer, and never block a PATCH. A `ranges` PATCH whose own edit would no longer land because the band plan changed since the job was read is refused instead (`range-locked`, `reason: "range-plan-changed"`), leaving the job unchanged; read the job again and resend. Before SF1 a `ranges` key in a PATCH was ignored; a client that sends its create body back now gets `range-locked`, so leave `ranges` out unless you mean to edit them.

### Signals only

`signalsOnly: true` makes a discover job find and classify signals without starting decoder trials of its own: no confirm or follow probes. Detection, classification, discoveries, evidence and burst captures work as usual, and identities decoded by configured (ambient) decoders or by other jobs on the same frequency are still attached. A signals-only job never widens the protocols other jobs probe. Use it to map activity without spending CPU on decoders. Monitor jobs cannot use it (`signals-only-unsupported`). It can be switched on and off with a PATCH, effective as soon as the PATCH returns; a probe already holding a signal finishes its hold.

Three ready-to-send examples:

1. DMR in PMR446, in-window: `{ "ranges": [{"startHz":446000000,"endHz":446200000}], "protocols": ["dmr"] }`.
2. 400–470 MHz sweep with takeover and recording: `{ "mode": "sweep", "bandplans": ["pmr446","uhf-land-mobile"], "takeover": true, "record": {"voice": true} }`.
3. A one-hop "move there and watch" job: `{ "mode": "sweep", "ranges": [{"startHz":145750000,"endHz":145850000}], "stop": {"afterMs": 600000} }`.

### Lifecycle

```
pending ─► starting ─► running ⇄ holding
               │           │  ▲
               │           ▼  │
               │         paused(reason) ──► running (resume / auto-resume)
               ▼           │
   failed ◄── any non-terminal ──► cancelled        running/holding ──► completed (stop condition)
```

`completed`, `cancelled` (reason `source-paused` when a paused receiver feed ended an identify job) and `failed` (`source-removed`, `tuner-unavailable`, `invalid`) are terminal; only terminal jobs can be deleted. Pause reasons:

- `operator`: paused by `POST …/pause`; probes stop, the lease and the decoder hold are kept until an operator or relay write takes the tuner, which drops both (the job stays `paused(operator)`, it never auto-resumes).
- `preempted-operator`: a REST tuner write (`origin: "rest"`) on the leased source; the lease is lost and never restored over. Any REST write preempting applies to the individual routes: `POST /api/tuner/:sourceId/tune` refuses with 409 unless `preempt` is true.
- `preempted-relay`: an SDR++ relay client took control (`control-mode-changed` → `external`).
- `preempted-job`: a higher-priority scanner job took the lease (it keeps the original snapshot). The waiting job keeps its decoder hold while that job holds the tuner; when an operator or relay write takes it, the waiter releases the decoders too, and holds them again when the lease comes back.
- `source-down`: the source disconnected; the job resumes on the next payload with a new epoch.
- `resumed-without-retune`: the receiver feed was paused and then resumed plainly (without restoring its tuning; another client such as SDR++ may be using the receiver). A sweep that would otherwise take the tuner on its own gets this reason too: one paused by the feed, one waiting to auto-resume (`preempted-operator`, `preempted-relay`, `preempted-job`) and one due to resume after a restart (`resume.onBoot`); `paused(operator)` keeps its reason, and in-window and passive jobs resume as usual. The sweep writes no tuning and does not hop; one paused by the feed keeps the lease (so no waiting job takes the tuner and hops instead); each releases its decoder hold, so the source's decoders run on whatever the receiver is tuned to. It never resumes on its own, also across a restart; `resume` (Resume scan) retunes to its current hop. Its hops then run without a band gain (a job gain is still written), and nothing is restored when it ends (the pre-pause tuning and the gain saved before a band gain are forgotten). An operator or relay write, or another job taking the lease, meanwhile drops the lease without changing the reason: when that job ends, the sweep stays held.
- `core-restart`: the job was running when the core stopped; it resumes only with `resume.onBoot` (passive jobs always do).
- `resume-blocked`: auto-resume found more to disturb than was consented.

Auto-resume: once the preemptor leaves (relay control back to `user`, and no REST tuner write for `resume.autoAfterIdleMs`, 60 s by default) the job re-checks its impact. If it is not larger than what was consented, it re-takes the lease and resumes at the same hop; otherwise it goes to `paused(resume-blocked)` and needs `resume` with `takeover: true`. An explicit `resume` from `paused(preempted-*)` arbitrates at once; `takeover` is not required (sending it records the current impact as consented for later auto-resumes). It answers 409 `SCANNER_SOURCE_BUSY` while another job holds the lease or a better waiter exists.

Automatic resume after operator or relay preemption is held by active identify trials on that source and, when an `OperatorActivityPort` is injected, by operator audio or recording. The default composition injects live audio only: it counts while a client listens through a gating squelch (manual threshold below 0, or auto) on the job's source; recording activity is not reported yet. Audio adds a 3-second hang; the full idle interval starts after activity/hang ends. Job REST and `scanner:job` views expose `resumeAt` (ISO deadline or null) and `resumeHeldBy` (`audio`, `identify`, `recording`, or null). An elapsed `resumeAt` reads as the time it was first seen elapsed and stays fixed while resume is pending. Job-to-job lease hand-back is unaffected by these activity holds. Without an operator port, no external audio/recording activity is inferred. `POST …/pause` on `paused(preempted-*)` means **Stay here**: it becomes `paused(operator)` without acquiring the lease and remains explicitly paused across restart, even with `onBoot` set.

A job in `paused(preempted-job)` shows `preemptedBy: { jobId, name }`, the job that holds its tuner now (`name` null when that job has none). It is absent in every other state and when no job holds the lease, and the paused job's `scanner:job` event is sent again when the holder changes.

When job-level `protocols` is omitted, runtime probes and preview use the union of the enabled ranges covering that signal/hop. A neighbouring range's DMR preference does not apply to a Marine VHF signal. Explicit job-level protocols, including an explicit empty list (all), override range preferences.

Explicit `resume` in the other cases:

- `paused(operator)` with the lease: resumes at the same hop.
- `paused(operator)` after an operator or relay write took the lease: re-acquires it. With a grown impact and no `takeover` it answers 409 `SCANNER_TAKEOVER_REQUIRED` (the job stays paused); while a job of equal or higher priority holds the lease it answers 409 `SCANNER_SOURCE_BUSY` and moves to `paused(resume-blocked)` (taking the lease from a lower-priority job needs consent like a takeover).
- `paused(resumed-without-retune)`: re-holds the decoders and retunes to the current hop; without the lease it re-acquires it as from `paused(operator)`.
- `paused(core-restart)`: re-acquires the lease, re-opens the engine and restarts the sweep from its first hop.

Every 409 `SCANNER_SOURCE_BUSY` carries `details.reason`: `releasing`, `recovery-pending` and `holder-starting` clear by themselves (retry); `relay` and `held` last until the relay or the holding job lets go.

### Takeover and impact

A sweep that would disturb something answers 409 `SCANNER_TAKEOVER_REQUIRED` with `details.impact`: `liveAudioClients`, `digitalVoiceListeners`, `runningDecoders`, `pinnedDecoders` (started by hand), `scannerJobs` (other non-passive jobs on the source) and `unrestorable` tuner fields. Resending with `takeover: true` consents. Decoders on the source are held (`suspension.reasonCode: "tuner-scanning"`) for the whole sweep and resume afterwards; a manual start of a held decoder answers 409 `SOURCE_HELD_BY_SCANNER`. Live-audio listeners hear the sweep. Relay clients (SDR++) holding the tuner make sweeps impossible (`SCANNER_SOURCE_BUSY`). A paused receiver feed (`POST /api/sources/:id/pause`) takes no new job, identify request or job resume (409 `SOURCE_PAUSED`); jobs running or still starting at the pause wait in `paused` with reason `source-down` (a starting job is not failed, unlike a dropout during startup). A restore resume (`restoreTuning: true`, Resume at …) continues them, where the usual reconnect recovery restores a lease's tuning and band gain; a plain resume writes no tuning and leaves a waiting sweep in `paused(resumed-without-retune)` until you resume it. Identify jobs do not wait: they end `cancelled(source-paused)`.

### Tuner restore

At the end of a sweep the scanner writes back only the fields it changed, and only those whose previous value was known; others are listed as `unrestorable`. A restore that cannot be written (the source is disconnected, or the first retune failed half-way) is kept and runs on the source's first data after the reconnect, also when the job was deleted in between. A lease lost to an operator or relay is never restored over. After a crash the previous tuning is restored on the source's first data. The spectrum engine runs with the lease holder's FFT size, integration and threshold, and returns to the defaults when no sweep holds the source. On shutdown the scanner hook runs before decoders, channelizer, fanout and sources stop, so the restore (bounded at 2 s) still reaches the rtl_tcp socket.

## Discoveries, evidence and storage

One discovery per emitter: same frequency key, protocol family and identity. Two DMR networks on one channel are two discoveries, cross-linked as co-channel. An observation surfaces after its channel gate passes, or after an accepted identity decode places a short burst. Observations are kept per transmission with signal statistics and the decode evidence.

### Detection quality: admission, association and data bursts

Track snapshots include updates at blocks 2, 5 and 20, then the existing periodic cadence. The block-5 class can make a visit hold earlier, by design; dwell settings and acceptance gates are unchanged.

A discovery means something transmitted there. These rules keep intermod products, noise and pager bursts out of the list and keep one emitter on one record. Full rules, fixtures and measurements: [detection quality spec](specs/detection-quality/requirements.md).

**Admission.** An observation that no decode proves creates a discovery only with evidence of a signal:

- a channel SNR of at least 18 dB, or at least 25 dB when the front end was overloaded (`overload` flag; intermod products of a clipping carrier stay below it);
- otherwise (not overloaded, under 18 dB) it is held as pending, not stored or shown. It is promoted when a later sighting reaches 18 dB, or when 3 distinct observations within 10 min of the first have a median SNR of at least 15 dB. Overloaded weak sightings never enter pending;
- a signal that stays on is promoted by duration: one sighting whose channel SNR has a median of at least 12 dB over at least 3 s, with no reading under 9 dB and no gap over 2.5 s between readings, creates a discovery. This is how a broadcast FM station or a steady carrier at 10 to 17 dB gets a record, since it is one long sighting rather than several short ones. Noise does not stay at 12 dB for 3 s;
- a TDMA carrier (class `tdma-ms`, DMR-like) keys only about half the time, so its channel SNR reads about 3 dB under its real level: its readings count 3 dB higher (10 · log10(1 / 0.5) = 3.01 dB) toward that 12 dB median. A reading under 9 dB still ends its run, so in practice a `tdma-ms` sighting that stays at 9 dB or more for 3 s gets a record: a 10 dB DMR carrier (readings 9 to 12 dB) does, a 6 dB one (readings 6 to 8 dB) does not;
- push-to-talk use adds up: when separate transmissions on one channel (for example 2 to 10 s airband overs) each last past their first second, their airtime is pooled for 60 s, and the channel gets a record once the pooled airtime reaches 3 s with a median of at least 12 dB. Adjacent 8.33 kHz channels pool separately, and any new record on the channel, however it was created, clears its pooled airtime;
- a short burst (under 1 s) needs its own signal level, not just its strongest channel reading, to reach 15 dB before it creates a record at once; a weaker one waits for more sightings like any weak signal, so a single weak data burst does not leave a one-sighting record;
- the detector looks at 95 % of the tuned span (±486.4 kHz at 1.024 Msps), but signals beyond the usable 80 % (±409.6 kHz) are flagged `edge` on their observations: such a signal passes the same channel gate, needs its own signal level at 15 dB to create a record at once however long it lasts, shows as class `unknown`, and is never held to confirm, IQ-captured, burst-captured by a capture job, given PSD evidence or given an automatic analog trial there. An IQ or burst capture whose signal turns `edge` after it started is discarded, never replayed or kept (a replay already running then has its result dropped: no identity, no trial memory) (probes and analog trials keep their whole channel inside the usable 80 %). Retune to put it inside the 80 % to identify it;
- a signal is judged in a bandwidth that matches its own width: a signal wider than one channel by its strongest channel, and a narrow carrier by its own few kHz rather than diluted across a whole 8.33 or 25 kHz channel;
- an observation with a missing or non-finite centre or SNR is dropped;
- a receiver spur that moves with the tuner is flagged, never deleted. A steady narrow line (a carrier line: at most 4 FFT bins wide at -20 dB, not a narrow-digital flat top; over 4 blocks and on at least 90 % of the time, so keyed, bursty or modulated signals never are) is a receiver spur at an offset from the tuned centre when, within the last 10 min and at one sample rate, it was seen at that offset (within 1.2 kHz, widths within a factor of 1.5, each counted as at least 3 kHz) from at least 3 distinct tuned centres (at least 12.5 kHz apart; a sweep revisiting its hops keeps the evidence fresh but each centre counts once), and nothing contradicts it: no line at that offset had evidence of a signal or read as not steady, no such line's frequency was seen from another centre whose usable window (the usable 80 %, more than 12.5 kHz from its centre) covered it (one frequency seen from two centres is a real transmitter), and no stay of at least 3 s whose usable window covered the offset saw nothing there. The windows need not overlap, so a sweep whose hops tile the band (for example 3 hops 785 kHz apart at 1.024 Msps) qualifies at its third hop; two centres never do, since two real transmitters can sit at one offset from two centres. A flat line exactly 3 FFT bins wide at -20 dB and at -6 dB is ambiguous (a tone on a bin centre whose neighbours noise lifted, or a narrow-digital flat top such as dPMR or NXDN48 read the same way): it is no carrier (narrow digital keeps its trials), and as a sighting it counts neither for nor against a spur. The spectrum engine's own spur lines (a line on for 30 s at one offset is masked before tracking) count as such sightings 2 s into each stay, so the records a spur left before the mask are flagged too. Those 2 s run on the stay's clock, which also advances through blocks lost to a gap, overload or blanking; the engine demotes a spur line only after 1.5 s of off time in observed blocks, so a mask line left over from before a retune could count once if most of those 2 s were unobserved (a known limit). Flagging marks the records that were only that line (`receiverSpur` on the discovery, with the offset, sample rate, centre count and time): the list hides them unless asked (`includeReceiverSpurs=true`, see [API.md](API.md#get-apiscannerdiscoveries)), and nothing is deleted. A record is flagged only when the scanner saw its whole history in this run (or it was restored with a saved flag, below): one restored after a restart without a flag, one joined by an observation without its tuned centre, one created by such an observation that did not read as the same steady line until its tuned centre came, one that ever measured something other than a steady line, or one that absorbed another in a merge is never flagged, nor is one that ever had a decode, identity, proof, recording or IQ evidence, operator label, note, Ignore or Keep, or a kept recording, even if that was later removed; a flagged record that gets any of these is unflagged for good. Flagged records are left out of the per-job discovery counts too. A learned offset expires 10 min after it was last proven (its flags lift with it: a flag stands only while its offset is learned), and is revoked at once (no spur there for 10 min, its flags lifted) on contradicting evidence: a sighting at it is decoded or proven or is not a steady narrow line, one frequency at that offset is seen from two centres, or a stay of at least 3 s whose usable window covered the offset ends (the tuner moves on) having seen nothing there. Every flag set or lifted is published as a discovery update, including one a read finds no longer standing. Flags are saved with their records: after a restart (learned offsets start empty) a saved flag stays set, the record hidden, and holds if the same offset (same source and sample rate) is learned again within 10 min; otherwise it lifts then. A record once shown to be more than a spur (any of the above while flagged) is saved as such and never flagged again, across restarts. The scanner keeps up to 512 records per source whose history it saw; one dropped past that bound is never flagged, and a warning is logged the first time it happens.

A further rule for weak repeating transmitters is implemented but **off** (`DQ_REPEAT_ENABLED` in `src/core/scanner/detection-quality-contract.ts`): 3 distinct observations on one channel within 60 s, each with a local SNR of at least 6 dB and a channel SNR of at least 9 dB, their median at least 10 dB. On the synthetic full scene it adds 10 dB AFSK1200, OOK and LoRa and a 6 dB POCSAG burst with no false discovery. The synthetic noise scenes give no gate-passed sighting at all, so they cannot show how often real noise repeats on a channel. On recorded band captures the rule added about a fifth more records (wide, unidentified), and without ground truth they cannot be told apart from noise. It stays off until it is measured against real captures with a known answer.

A decoded observation is admitted as before. The channel gate counts only blocks at least 3 dB over the floor toward its minimum on-time (300 ms, 100 ms for a burst). On airband, the gate measures 8.33 kHz channels, so an AM voice carrier is judged in its own channel and not diluted by a 25 kHz one (about 4.8 dB better). In a band plan range that lists AM (airband, CB), a carrier that shows speech sidebands (a -20 dB width of 6 to 12.5 kHz) gets the class `analog-am` (AM voice) and is tried with AM first, not labelled a steady carrier. A plain carrier is still a carrier, and a very weak AM carrier whose sidebands are lost in the noise still looks like one.

**Steady carriers are not voice.** A track is a `carrier` when it is at most 2 FFT bins wide at -6 dB, and also when it is a steady carrier line that drift, phase noise or a centre on a bin widens past that, longer than 4 blocks and on at least 90 % of the time (keyed CW at about half duty and data bursts are not). A carrier line is counted in bins of the track's own FFT (sample rate / FFT size: 1 000 Hz at 1.024 Msps, 1 171.875 Hz at 2.4 Msps), not in Hz: at most 4 bins at -20 dB (a pure tone reads 3 bins on a bin centre and 4 half a bin off), and by shape: at most 2 bins at -6 dB; or peaked with at most 3 bins at -6 dB; or flat but at most 2 bins at -6 dB and 3 bins at -20 dB. Narrow digital voice (dPMR, NXDN48, about 4 kHz with a flat top) can read as narrow as 3 bins at -20 dB and at -6 dB, the widths of a 3-bin tone, so a flat line 3 bins wide at -6 dB keeps its digital trials (a tone that noise made read flat does too: when unsure, not a carrier), as does a line whose -6 dB width is not known; FM voice measures 7.5 kHz or more. A carrier strong enough to open an FM squelch produces an audio "transmission" with no voice in it; when the transmission's own observation measured a `carrier` within that 4-bin width (of its own FFT; 1 kHz bins when not known), such an NFM transmission or analog trial without a CTCSS tone or DCS code counts as carrier evidence (`carrier`, activity), not as an `analog-fm` candidate. The observation decides, not the record's history: an FM over on a frequency that was a quiet carrier before still counts as voice. A tone still proves an FM transmitter, and AM rows are unchanged.

**Early analog classification.** A detection IQ capture of a track classed `analog-fm` ends once the track has been on for 2 s rather than at the 4 s capture limit, so the analog trial (tone and voice) answers while a long over is still on: a 28 s voice ID at 20 dB is identified about 3 s after it starts instead of 4.5 s (synthetic benchmark). The trial needs 0.75 s of audio for a tone; 2 s plus the 500 ms pre-roll leaves margin. Analog evidence from live audio (a listener on the channel) arrives when the transmission closes, or earlier, once, when its CTCSS tone or DCS code qualifies on 0.75 s of audio while it is still on; the close then adds nothing more. If the store refuses that early row, the close records as usual. Known gap: the data-burst check runs on the close, so a data burst that carries a CTCSS tone and lasts past 0.75 s gets its early tone row as voice evidence, and that row stays.

**One signal, one segment.** Before signals become tracks, each spectrum block is cut into signals:

- at a dip of 6 dB or more below the peaks on both sides, when the dip also reaches the lower half of the weaker side's height above the noise floor (so the two tones of one FSK pager are not split);
- at the channel edges of the band plans that cover the tuned window, also while only the background (passive) scan runs; each band plan's channels apply inside its own range only;
- pieces that join without a 6 dB dip never cross a channel edge of the band plan's widest channel spacing (25 kHz on airband, 12.5 kHz on land mobile), so a crowded band splits into one signal per channel. A piece more than 10 dB weaker than a neighbour peaking within 31.25 kHz (AM sidebands, splatter) stays with that neighbour, and two peaks closer than 0.8 of a channel stay one signal;
- without a band plan (FM broadcast, for example) only the dip rule applies, so a broadcast station stays one signal.

A track's reported bandwidth never exceeds its widest single block, so a weak track that drifts does not grow into its neighbours.

**One channel, one record.** During the background scan, a discovery on a band with a channel plan is placed on its channel (the finest channel spacing of the band plan that is at least half the signal's width: 8.33 kHz for an airband voice carrier, 25 kHz for a wider data burst): sightings whose measured centre wanders inside one channel join one record, adjacent channels stay separate records, and such a signal is never labelled `wideband`. A sighting creates no record when it does not stand at least 6 dB above the neighbouring 12.5 kHz on each side (a raised stretch of busy channels or splatter), or when its nearer edge sits within 12.5 kHz of the tuned centre, where the receiver's own DC pedestal shows. A decode still creates a record in both cases.

**Association.** One record per emitter:

- a sighting of the same family as a record joins it within the usual frequency tolerance;
- a sighting within 1 kHz of a record on the same source joins it whatever family the classifier gave it, when its occupied bandwidth lies between a third of the narrowest and three times the widest of the record's last 64 sightings (half and twice for a record that decoded or carries an identity). So a DMR signal classed analog-fm one time and fsk4 the next, or a transmitter whose measured width swings from 13 to 39 kHz, stays one discovery from its first sighting on; a plain carrier joins its own record the same way;
- for association, a record's family counts only when it decoded, carries an identity or has proof (a decode, heard analog audio or carrier-only evidence). A protocol guess alone (a candidate pocsag, dmr or analog-fm label) counts as unknown, so it never keeps records apart; the label you see is unchanged;
- a sighting decoded as one protocol never joins a record decoded as another (DMR and P25, POCSAG and FLEX);
- a record lists at most 8 co-channel partners, nearest first; partners that no longer exist or have drifted out of tolerance are dropped on both sides;
- a DMR, P25 or YSF talker id decoded on a record 6 to 30 kHz from the record that already holds it, at the same time and at least 10 dB weaker, is an adjacent-channel copy and stays on the stronger record.

**Duplicate records are folded together.** Two records on the same source whose median centres are within 1 kHz, each with a median bandwidth inside the other's bandwidth range (above) and not split by the level rule below, are one emitter and are merged, unless they carry different identities (two DMR colour codes), are two different confirmed families (a proven analog record and its decoded digital twin), are both decoded as different protocols (DMR and P25 included), or either has an operator label, note, Ignore or Keep. The oldest record survives (a decoded one first); counts, airtime, evidence and recordings move to it and the merged-away id becomes one of its aliases, like any other merge (its own URL then answers 404). This runs once at every core start, before scanning resumes, on at most the 32 records nearest each candidate (a group holds at most 16 records; anything left over is folded at the next start), and again whenever a sighting joins a record that has such a duplicate next to it. A start that is interrupted while folding finishes the same groups at the next start (`consolidation-plan.json` in the scanner state directory, removed once a snapshot covers the result).

**Fragments of one channel.** WaveKit folds the fragments older versions left beside or inside one channel, and retires stale ones. Before it removes a record it confirms that no evidence folder, digital voice or analog recording folder, burst capture (finished or still being written), evidence write in progress or remembered replay trial exists for it or any of its aliases; a folder it cannot check (for example a permission error) counts as present, so the record is kept. Every 5 minutes, with the inactive prune:

- records on one channel of the band plan's channel grid (the finest channel raster at that frequency, within half a channel spacing of the channel centre, and no wider than two channels) are merged into that channel's record, even when they are more than 1 kHz apart; two records merge only when they also share a channel of the finest grid at either of them, so a wide record never swallows a neighbouring narrow channel;
- the channel record is the one with an operator label, note, Ignore or Keep, else one with a decode or evidence, else the one with the most sightings (then the oldest). A record with an operator label, note, Ignore or Keep, a decode, decoded data (talkgroups, capcodes, callsigns, an identity), recordings, PSD or IQ evidence, proof, a kept recording, or any evidence the check finds, is never merged away or retired;
- merges follow the same rules as above (identity, confirmed family, decoded protocol and level split keep records apart), move sightings, airtime, first and last seen and metadata to the channel record, and the merged-away id resolves to it; a merge that would give a record more than 64 aliases is refused;
- a record with at most 2 sightings, never classified or decoded and not seen for 72 hours retires (it is deleted, like an inactive prune);
- one pass looks at up to 512 records, merges at most 32 groups of at most 16 records (it stops looking once 32 formed), retires at most 256 records and runs the evidence check at most 1 024 times; the next pass continues where it stopped. N records holding G groups of fragments and R stale records are all handled within N / 512 + G / 32 + R / 256 passes (each rounded up); the worst case for a 10 000-record store, 5 000 two-record fragments, is 20 + 157 = 177 passes (about 15 hours), while real stores, where a few per cent of records are fragments, clear in a few passes. Each merge and retirement is an ordinary journaled merge or delete, so an older WaveKit reading the store afterwards sees the result and nothing new.

**Strong overs next to ambient carriers.** A sighting at least 20 dB stronger or weaker than a record and at least 1.5 times wider or narrower does not attach to it; it gets its own record. Both differences are needed, so a fading mobile or a noise record whose width swings stays one record. With 3 or more observations the record's medians are compared; a younger record is compared sighting by sighting. This applies to every merge path. An overloaded sighting never raises a record's best level, and a record's class is the majority of its last 64 observations, not the latest one.

**Pager data is not voice.** A closed live-audio transmission is not stored as an analog voice (`analog-transmission`) Activity row when Activity accepted a paging or APRS decode from the same source within 1 250 Hz of its frequency, during the transmission or up to 1 s before or after it, on a channel whose tuning WaveKit commands or relays. The pager decode is the record of that burst, and identify attaches no analog proof for it (no `analog-fm` candidate). The decision waits 1 s after the final close is published, so voice rows are stored one second later than before: an ordinary squelch close is already final only after the 1.5 s merge gap, so its row appears about 2.5 s after the audio ends. The `live-audio:transmission` WebSocket events are unchanged. When anything is unknown (no provenance, unverified tuning, more than 8 closes waiting) or at shutdown, the transmission is stored as voice, as before.

**Decodes reach the discovery.** A protocol that confirms on two matching decodes (a POCSAG or FLEX capcode, an APRS address, an rtl_433 device, a DMR colour code, an AIS, ACARS or VDL2 identity) pairs them across every probe and configured decoder on the same discovery (or, before it has one, the same observation): one decode in each of two short probes confirms. The two decodes must be at least 2 s and at most 5 min apart, so one transmission heard by two decoders at once counts once; a repeat within 5 min refreshes the window. At most 4096 identities are remembered; the oldest go first. A replayed IQ capture still pairs only within its own replay. A configured decoder's decode names tracks by occupied-span containment with a small placement tolerance (see Ambient identification below). Ambiguous signals receive no identity. Activity rows from scanner probes carry the probe's channel frequency, rtl_433 rows its own `freq` estimate when it lies in 24 to 1766 MHz and within the usable 80 % of the input it heard (0.8 × sample rate / 2 of its channel, ±409.6 kHz at 1.024 Msps), else its channel frequency (also when the input rate is unknown), and readsb rows 1090 MHz.

**One record per ACARS or VDL2 channel.** ACARS and VDL2 channels lie on the 25 kHz raster, and a confirmed, CRC-clean ACARS or VDL2 decode from a `commanded` or `relay` source lands on its channel's one record. A record is that channel's when it lies within 4 kHz of it (3 999 Hz is, 4 000 Hz is not; less than half the 8.33 kHz spacing, so a neighbouring channel's record never is). A channel anchor, the record such a decode opens where no record exists, owns its channel: no spectrum discovery opens within 4 kHz of it, a live observation there stays with the anchor, and every later decode at the channel is the anchor's. With no anchor, the stored spectrum discovery at the channel (the one already decoded as the protocol, else the only one in reach) is the owner and takes the decode, from any epoch: the decode lands on the owner's newest observation when that observation pairs with it in time (from 2 s before its start to 2 s after its end), else it proves the owner as a channel proof in memory. When the reach holds two or more records and none is decoded as the protocol, or the owner's tuning is not trusted or it is decoded as another protocol, the decode opens an anchor instead. A restored owner (after a restart) shows as unproven, at its last protocol as a candidate, until a fresh confirmed decode; identify does not answer from it.

To find discoveries by what a decoder reported, `GET /api/scanner/discoveries?identity=<kind>:<value>` (for example `identity=dmr-cc:1` or `identity=pocsag-capcode:1234567`) keeps those whose identity has that kind and value, ignoring case and surrounding spaces. The free-text `q` matches the operator label or the identity value; talkgroups, callsigns and other metadata are not searched. See [API.md](API.md#get-apiscannerdiscoveries).

Discoveries read through `GET /api/scanner/discoveries` carry `coverage` when decoder status is known: up to 3 decoders whose placements cover the discovery's frequency on one of its sources, with their state (`running`, `suspended` with its reason, or `stopped`). A channel placement covers its channel centre plus or minus half the channel width the decoder is placed with (`channelWidthHz` on the decoder status placement), or 12.5 kHz when it reports none. See [API.md](API.md#discovery-coverage).

Storage layout under `scanner.storage.dir`:

- `discoveries.json` (snapshot), `journal/` (daily observation journals), `jobs.json`, `settings.json`;
- `engine/` (per-source detector state) with `engine/occupancy/` (occupancy history);
- `recordings/dv/<discoveryId>/` (digital voice WAVs, see [Digital voice recordings](#digital-voice-recordings)), `recordings/analog/<discoveryId>/` (analog voice WAVs, see [Analog voice recordings](#analog-voice-recordings)), `evidence/<discoveryId>/` (`psd.json`, `iq-*.cu8`);
- `captures/` (burst IQ captures);
- `iq-captures/` (protected mode with `iqCapture.enabled` only: short narrowband captures waiting for replay, deleted at their result; see [IQ capture on detection](#iq-capture-on-detection)).

Every store change is appended to the journal at once, so `discoveries.json` is only rewritten (compacted) to bound replay and let old journal days be pruned. A compaction is due once the journal written since the last one passes the snapshot's own size (at least 1 MiB) or half of `storage.maxJournalMb`, whichever is smaller, or once the store has changed and 24 h have passed; raising `maxJournalMb` therefore spaces compactions of a busy, large store further apart. A write budget of 1.5 GB a day sets the shortest gap between two: the snapshot's size x 86,400 s / 1.5 GB (86.4 min at 90 MB, 10 s for a store under about 170 KB), so a burst of activity or a tiny `maxJournalMb` cannot rewrite the store more often. At today's typical rate (about 655 B/s of journal, 57 MB a day) a 90 MB store compacts about 1.7 times a day: about 0.21 GB written a day, journal included, where the size-scaled cadence before wrote 2.06 GB. The journal since the last compaction, which a restart replays, is then up to 32 MiB at the default cap. Start-up, consolidation and shutdown snapshots are written whenever they happen. A journal that has to refuse lines (backlog full or a failed write) refuses every change, recordings included, until a snapshot covers it, so that snapshot starts within 1 s whatever the store's size. If the journal keeps refusing while snapshots succeed, the sixth hole snapshot in a row waits 2 s and each further one twice as long, up to 5 min (2, 4, 8 … 256 s, then 300 s) and never longer than the write-budget gap (10 s for a small store); a hole that comes 10 min or more after the last hole snapshot starts over at 1 s. A failed snapshot is retried after 10 s, then 20, 40, 80 s and so on, up to 2 h but never sooner than the write-budget gap (86.4 min at 90 MB), or up to 5 min while a hole waits; the first success resets it. A journal-size snapshot the main thread must build (snapshot worker unavailable) also waits 1 s per MiB of snapshot (88 s at 88 MiB) when that is longer. Any snapshot the main thread builds, a hole's included and a snapshot worker's fallback included, also starts no sooner than nine times its last main-thread build after that build ended (24.3 s after a 2.7 s build), so recurring holes keep main-thread snapshots under 10 % of the core's time; the first hole after a quiet spell is still covered within 1 s, and snapshots the worker builds are not delayed. Snapshot timing runs on a monotonic clock, so setting the system clock back does not delay snapshots. A crash or restart replays the journal written since the last snapshot. The journal is read in 256 KiB pieces: lines the snapshot already covers are recognised by their leading `seq` and skipped without being parsed, so a large day file the snapshot covers costs a byte scan, and both the read and the replay hand the event loop back every 8 ms. A torn final line (a crash mid-write) is cut off and counted as dropped. A missing `seq` (a corrupt or lost line, or journal days pruned under a snapshot that was later found invalid) withholds only that line: every valid line after it is still replayed in `seq` order, recovery is reported degraded, and the start-up log names the first missing `seq` and how many are missing. New changes are written at once, and the next snapshot keeps what was replayed. Day files whose `seq` runs backwards (the system clock was set back between days) are put in order and replayed normally.

**Downgrading after the `analog-am` class.** This version stores AM voice discoveries with class `analog-am`. An older WaveKit does not know that class: it rejects the whole `discoveries.json` snapshot (moved aside as invalid) and every journal mutation that names it, so its AM records are lost and the rest is rebuilt from the journal alone. Before upgrading a host you may need to roll back, stop WaveKit and back up `scanner.storage.dir`; to downgrade, stop the newer version and restore that backup before starting the older one. Anything discovered since the backup is not kept.

Retention: the `storage.max*Mb` caps, `maxAgeDays` (recordings, evidence and journal files), `discoveryMaxAgeDays` (discoveries not marked `keep`) and `maxDiscoveries`. When free disk drops below `minFreeDiskMb` the status reports `disk: "disk-low"`: recording and evidence stop; discoveries keep updating. Privacy: identifiers (talkgroups, radio ids, callsigns, capcodes) stay on the host; nothing is uploaded.

### Digital voice recordings

A scan job's dsd-fme probe with `record.voice` (the default) writes one WAV per call. When the probe stops, each call file moves to `recordings/dv/<discoveryId>/` of the observation it belongs to; `GET /api/recordings` lists it as kind `scanner-dv` with the discovery's frequency and the file's duration. A file belongs to an observation of the probe's track when its talkgroup and source match either the observation's latest decode, with the call starting within the observation's time (2 s slack), or one of the calls the probe reported inside it, with the call starting within that call's own time (2 s slack), so every over of a conversation is kept, not only the last radio's (at most 256 reported calls per probe, encrypted calls counted first; a probe that reported more than 256 encrypted calls keeps none of its files). A file that matches any encrypted call or encrypted observation is deleted, even when a clear call of the same pair also matches, as is any file while the disk is low. Every encrypted call the probe reported vetoes its own window (2 s slack), even one outside every observation's time. An encrypted call with no talkgroup or source vetoes every call file in its window, clear calls of other radios included (fail closed). A call still open when the probe stops (a `TEMP_` file) is deleted and the observation's recording is marked `cut`. The passive job does not probe unless `scanner.passive.offsetProbes` is on, so with the default configuration a passive in-window discovery gets no digital voice recording.

### Analog voice recordings

A recorder decision keeps the receipt outcome and a closed error code, so a store failure is told apart from a cancellation while the decision codes stay the same.

With the default configuration the scanner keeps every analog FM voice transmission it finds, passive in-window sightings and sweep holds alike, as one WAV (8 kHz, mono, 16-bit) per transmission under `recordings/analog/<discoveryId>/`. `GET /api/recordings` lists them as kind `scanner-analog` with the discovery's frequency and the file's duration. No channelizer and no channel tier are needed: the scanner demodulates the channel from its own wideband IQ ring.

- **What is recorded.** A narrow track (occupied bandwidth up to 16 kHz) classified `analog-fm`, on `commanded` or `relay` tuning. The recorder does not wait for the classifier: a clean, narrow track that is not a burst, in a range a band plan lists for `analog-fm` (or that no band plan range with protocols covers), starts a recording in memory as soon as it appears, from the pre-roll. Nothing is written or reserved on disk until the observation is classed `analog-fm` (in any snapshot, the closing one included, or by the recording's own audio, below) and the audio sounds like voice; then the file is written from its start, so a class that arrives late, or only when the track closes, still keeps the whole transmission. **The recording's own audio classes it too**, for the recorder only. The spectrum alone can read a weak or low-deviation NFM over as a narrow digital class for its whole length (as `gmsk-narrow` or `fsk4-6k25`, or burst-like `ook-burst` at its start), so such a class never bars the recording on its own: only a class the channel tier refined, or one backed by a verified decode of the observation, counts as definitely not analog. Instead the recording's own audio may class it, over one file's audio inside the track, when all of these hold: the voice check below passed; one standard CTCSS tone (one of the 50 EIA/TIA-603 tones) dominated the sub-audible band in at least 8 of the tone detector's 500 ms blocks (one every 250 ms), in at least 90 % of the blocks any tone won and in at least 40 % of all blocks (speech over the tone can take the others), never missing more than 8 blocks (2 s) in a row; the tone reads at most 2.5 kHz deviation (only a strong data fundamental reads more: alternating 2FSK at 200 baud is a 100 Hz square wave); and the audio sounds like speech: of at least 2 s of 50 ms frames (after 500 ms to settle), at least half rise and fall like syllables (the analog trial's syllabic index of at least 0.3) and at least 10 % of them also repeat at a voice's pitch (a normalised autocorrelation of at least 0.5 at a period of an 80 to 400 Hz fundamental). These hold for the file so far when the class is given, and the recorder checks them again over the whole file when it ends: a file that no longer passes then (5 s of speech followed by 11 s of a bare carrier, for example) is discarded (`discarded:unclassed`), and one being written is cancelled. That class only lets the recorder keep the file: it adds no tone evidence and no class or label to the discovery, and a later definite other class (a verified decode, the channel tier), on a snapshot or the close, still discards the recording and cancels a file being written. The speech test is what rejects data and noise: in synthetic tests alternating 2FSK (200 and 134 baud, ±600 and ±2400 Hz), random 4FSK at 4800 baud and POCSAG-like data at 512 and 1200 baud are never classed (their level is steady), nor are a carrier with only a CTCSS tone fading at a syllabic rate under noise, or noise modulation keyed at a syllabic rate over a tone (they rise and fall, but FM noise never repeats at a pitch: no such frame reached 0.5), while syllabic voice over 67, 100 and 254.1 Hz is (83 to 100 % of its frames periodic). A weak synthetic voice (one pitched syllable in six, the others noise-like, about 12 dB above the noise in 12.5 kHz) has 16 to 20 % of its frames periodic, against the 10 % required, and one with one pitched syllable in twenty 5 % (not classed); of the synthetic data signals that rise and fall at a syllabic rate over a tone, POCSAG-like 1200 baud data keyed at 2 Hz reached about 6 %, and 4FSK, FFSK and AFSK1200 keyed at 4 Hz under 1 %. It is a heuristic with limits: a periodic waveform keyed on and off at a syllabic rate over a steady standard sub-audible tone is classed (a square wave switched at 4 Hz, DTMF digits, a five-tone selcall such as ZVEI, a CW ID, or AFSK keyed at 3 Hz, each over a CTCSS tone), a voice that does not rise and fall (a steady test tone) is not, and neither is a voice with no CTCSS tone read as digital for its whole length (a known limit), or an over shorter than about 3 s. A recording classed only by its own audio keeps a low slot priority: a track classed `analog-fm` takes its slot whenever every slot is held. Any other waiting track (a shadow, digital class or not) takes it once the recording has heard 8 s of audio inside its track, unless the recording is shielded: its voice is on air (a voiced window in the last 2 s) and it is the oldest such recording on its source (one at most with the default 2 recordings per source, none with 1). A shielded recording keeps its slot until it has heard 60 s. Keyed data over a tone also reads as voice on air, so recordings classed only by their own audio never keep every slot from a waiting track: with two of them, the newer one yields after about 9 s (1 s judging delay included). A weak voice over shorter than 60 s that is shielded is recorded to its end; a longer one, or one that is not shielded (a second recording of this kind on the source), can lose its slot to a waiting carrier or data track, keeping its file so far. It then ends (`evicted`) and keeps its file so far, which is still checked over its whole length and ends with its own decision (`finalized`, or `discarded:unclassed`). Its observation is not refused: for up to 3 s it waits for the track it gave its slot to, then a later snapshot of it may start a recording again. With no other track waiting it records on. A shadow of a track with such a digital spectral class has the lowest slot priority: any track classed `analog-fm`, or a shadow of a track without a digital class, takes its slot when every slot is held. Another digital track does not, with two exceptions where any waiting track takes the slot first: the shadow's file can no longer be classed by its own audio (no standard CTCSS tone won its blocks for more than 8 blocks, 2 s, in a row, so at the earliest after 2.5 s of audio, 3.5 s with the judging delay), or the same observation's shadow already ended unclassed in an earlier visit (after that, it is recorded only while no other track waits, so a data signal keeps others out of a slot for its class window once, not on every visit of a sweep). Such a shadow then ends (`evicted`) as if its window had run out. It is discarded when it has not sounded like voice within 3 s of audio inside the track, or is not classed within 12 s (an unclassed data signal holds a slot once per visit, for at most about 13 s with the 1 s judging delay; its observation starts no other shadow in that visit, the same source stream and tuning, and only a later `analog-fm` snapshot starts a recording for it then, while a later visit of a sweep may shadow it again; one classed by its own audio is bounded as above). The synthetic voices of the tests, the weak one included, are classed 3.6 s after their onset (the earliest the 2.25 s of tone and 2 s of speech frames allow, with the judging delay); the 12 s window leaves room for an over whose pitched frames come later. While two digital shadows that can still be classed hold both slots, a weak voice read as digital waits for them (its start can then be cut, from the 3 s IQ ring). A recording still unclassed is not ended by time otherwise (its class may come only at the close), but it gives its slot to any track classed `analog-fm` that needs one, voiced or not (a narrow data signal such as 4FSK can sound like voice). A track closed without that class, or classed as something else (a decoded digital protocol, or a channel-tier class) before a snapshot classed it `analog-fm`, leaves nothing. A retune that ends such a recording before its class arrives keeps its voiced audio in memory for the track's closing snapshot, at most 2 s of signal after the retune (4 such recordings at most, the oldest dropped first); once every track that joined it has closed, a close classed `analog-fm` writes it (observation recording `cut`); an encrypted or opted-out close of any of them, anything else, or no close in time, drops it. At most 60 s of audio per recording waits in memory (960 KB); a recording whose file cannot be written by then (no discovery yet, or a store that does not take it) is dropped. Encrypted, edge, spur, IQ-image, truncated, wider and unverified-tuning tracks are never recorded. A recording is discarded when any track it joined turns encrypted (encrypted evidence that arrives only by the track's close counts too, even when that close brings the first `analog-fm` class) or unclean (edge, spur, IQ image, truncated, wider than 16 kHz), or loses its last covering job with `record.analog`, and when such a track appears on the recording channel (within 5 kHz). One exception: a spur or IQ-image flag first seen after the recording was classed and sounded like voice, and present on fewer than half of its snapshots so far (its start and every update and close of the tracks it joined), does not discard it; its files are written but give no tone evidence. A recording's tone evidence is published only once the recording has ended and all its files are written, and only if none of its snapshots was unclean, so the first file of a 120 s roll-over finalized before such a flag gives none either. Such a flag on half of the snapshots or more, one seen before the class and the voice, and every other flag still discard it. The decisions of a recording with an unclean snapshot carry `unclean`: which flag (`spur`, `iq-image`, `edge`, `truncated`, `wide` or `tuning`), which snapshot raised it (`snapshot` for an update of the recording's own track, `joined` for another track merged into it, `close` for a closing snapshot), when (`atMs`, ms of signal after the onset), how many of its snapshots were unclean (`flagged` of `snapshots`) and whether it was kept (`kept`); the decision's log line carries the same. The discard covers every file of the recording not yet written, the first file of a 120 s roll-over still being finished among them. A covering job with `record.analog: false` stops the recording unless another covering job (the passive job, for example) records analog; an observation no job covers is recorded.
- **Voice only.** A file is kept only if its audio inside the tracks (trimmed by 100 ms at each end, so pre-roll, hang and squelch noise do not count) has at least one 500 ms window that sounds like voice: louder than −36 dBFS above 500 Hz, with more energy at 500 to 1200 Hz than 1.4 times the energy above 1800 Hz. Audio too short for one window is judged on all of it when the file ends, if it lasts at least 200 ms: an over of 400 ms or more can be kept (a 600 ms over leaves 400 ms after the trims), a shorter one never is. A steady unmodulated carrier or a spur misclassified as `analog-fm`, and a carrier with only a CTCSS tone, leave no file. A recording that has heard 8 s of audio inside its tracks without one voice window ends, and that observation is not recorded again (a steady or idle carrier holds a slot for about 9 s at most, judged 1 s late); once a recording has sounded like voice, this no longer applies, so a silence of 8 s or more later in a conversation (after a 120 s roll-over, for example) does not end it. When every slot is held, a new track classed `analog-fm` takes a slot: that of an unclassed recording of a track with a digital spectral class first, then that of a recording classed only by its own audio (file being written or not), otherwise the oldest recording without a file being written that is either still unclassed (voiced or not) or has heard at least 2 s without sounding like voice, so neither steady carriers nor unclassed signals keep a voice out. A track not classed yet takes the slot of an unclassed digital-class recording when its own spectral class is not digital, and, whatever its spectral class, that of a recording classed only by its own audio past its hold (above). A voice that pauses for more than 8 s at the start of a transmission is lost. A CTCSS tone is never required. A carrier received under the FM threshold (about 3 dB over the channel noise) produces click noise that can pass.
- **Where a file starts and ends.** It starts `hold.preRollMs` (300 ms by default) before the onset, read back from the IQ ring. If the ring no longer holds that (more than 50 ms of it is missing), the file starts later and its observation recording is marked `cut` (`source-lost`). It never starts before the end of the channel's previous file or the epoch start. It ends `hold.hangMs` (2 s) after the last activity. A transmission on the same channel (within 5 kHz) that starts less than `hold.hangMs` after the previous one continues the same file; a later one starts a new file. A retune or a new source stream ends the file at the track's last sample (observation recording `cut`). A file reaching 120 s continues in a new one; the new file is admitted once the previous one is written, so a roll-over never needs a third recording slot (its audio waits in memory meanwhile, within the 60 s bound). Missing input of more than 1 s in total (a ring overrun on a loaded host) ends the file as `source-lost`; a shorter gap is written as silence. Decisions wait 1 s for late track reports, so audio reaches the disk about 1 s behind the air. At shutdown, open files are kept with cut `shutdown`, and files still waiting for their slot are written before the scanner stops.
- **Audio.** NFM with 750 µs de-emphasis and a 300 Hz high-pass (4th order) that removes a CTCSS sub-tone from the file. A standard CTCSS tone heard during a file's own transmission is attached to the observation as analog tone evidence (`proof.tone.ctcssHz`, proof kind `analog-tone`) when that file is written, if a snapshot classed the recording `analog-fm` (never for a recording classed only by its own audio, or kept over a late spur or image flag); each file is judged on its own audio.
- **Limits.** The storage caps, `minFreeDiskMb` and `maxAgeDays` apply as to every scanner recording; a recording refused at admission leaves no file, and is tried once more from the observation's next snapshot (a second refusal is final; a retried file that lost its start is `cut`). The demodulator costs 33 ms of a core per second of signal at 1.024 Msps and 49 ms at 2.4 Msps on a Mac at load average 7.5 (59 and 71 ms at load average 56). The voice check adds one filter chain per recording (four biquads per 8 kHz audio frame, whatever the IQ rate): the file's voice gate until it is voiced, then the per-transmission voice meter the sweep's voice hold reads (both run on the same audio only while a voiced recording's next file, after a 120 s roll-over, waits for its own gate); a recording that never sounds like voice runs no meter. A recording not classed yet, or classed only by its own audio, also runs the speech test of that class (three biquads per frame, a histogram per 50 ms frame, and for each frame that rises and falls like speech one pitch autocorrelation of up to 81 lags over at most 380 samples, about 31 000 multiply-adds, stopped at the first lag that is periodic enough): measured in one synthetic run of 60 s at load average 41 to 51, 1.3 to 2.0 ms of a core per second of audio for speech and 2.7 to 4.2 ms for noise that rises and falls (every lag searched), at most two such recordings per source. Measured in one synthetic run on a Mac at load average 5 to 6, with 1 and 2 voiced recordings at 1.024 and 2.4 Msps: the meter costs 0.34 to 0.53 ms of a core per second of audio per recording (before this change, with the gate and meter both filtering and a block allocated every 100 ms: 0.43 to 0.64 ms), 0.004 to 0.007 ms per audio judging step, about 75 a second per recording (0.005 to 0.008 ms before), about 1 % of the recording's 38 to 50 ms per second; it allocates nothing per slice or block. Each source runs at most floor(0.25 × `cpuBudgetCores` × 1000 / (50 + 9 × Msps)) recordings at once, at least 1 and at most 2 (2 with the default budget of 1 core); recordings held in memory count. Each recording also reserves its estimated cores (0.059 at 1.024 Msps, 0.072 at 2.4 Msps) in `cpuBudgetCores`, the budget probes and live-audio trials share, so recordings, probes and live audio are bounded together; a recording that does not fit waits for the next snapshot. The work runs on the main thread in slices of 25 ms of signal (about 1 ms each, 1.8 ms at most on the loaded host), as many per event-loop turn as fit in 2 ms (at least one). When a slow event loop lets a recording fall behind the live signal by more than the IQ ring less 1 s (less half the ring on rings under 2 s; 2 s with the default 3 s ring), the turn keeps demodulating until it is back within that bound, so a busy core delays a recording instead of losing it. **This catch-up is an explicit exception to the 2 ms main-thread rule** ([PERFORMANCE.md](PERFORMANCE.md) rule 2): losing the recording is worse than one long turn. It is bounded per event-loop turn across all recordings: one grant a turn, to the recording closest to falling out of the ring, of at most its margin of signal (1 s; half the ring on rings under 2 s). Every other recording stops at the 2 ms budget that turn and catches up on a later one, so two recordings never stack. A catch-up turn is therefore at most about 1 s of signal: about 60 ms of main thread at 1.024 Msps and 71 ms at 2.4 Msps on the loaded host, only while a recording is about to lose audio. Measured on a Mac at load average 55 to 60 with two recordings a full ring behind: the first turn caught up one of them (0.9 s of signal) while the other ran one 25 ms slice, and the next turn caught up the other; a warm catch-up turn of 0.7 s of signal took 47 to 49 ms, and the process's first one, before the JIT warmed up, took 106 ms (2.4 Msps) to 238 ms (1.024 Msps). A stretch of the ring that holds no IQ (overwritten, or a gap from dropped samples) costs no demodulation: the recorder steps through it 25 ms at a time in the same turn, never skipping readable IQ between gaps, and inserts silence; more than 1 s of consecutive missing signal ends the file (`source-lost`). A recording whose start has already left the ring starts at the ring's oldest sample (observation recording `cut`).
- **Recorder decisions.** Every decision the recorder takes about an observation has a reason code: `started` (a session classed `analog-fm`), `shadow` (a session started before its class), `classed` (by a snapshot, or by its own audio: speech over a stable CTCSS tone), `voiced` (the voice gate passed), `evicted` (its slot went to another track: to a track classed `analog-fm`, or, for a recording classed only by its own audio past its hold, to any waiting track; such a recording keeps its file so far, which still ends with its own decision; or, for a shadow not classed yet that its own audio can no longer class or whose observation already ended unclassed in an earlier visit, to any waiting track, nothing kept), `finalized` (a WAV was written), `refused:<why>` (not recorded) and `discarded:<why>` (audio was held and dropped without a file). Refusals: `channel-owned` (the channel tier records it), `no-iq` (the source's IQ is not `U8_IQ`), `tuning` (the track is not of the engine's current epoch, or its tuning is unverified), `not-analog` (no `analog-fm` class and no shadow allowed: a burst, a definite other class (decoded or refined by the channel tier), or a band plan range without analog FM), `slots` (every recording slot held), `cpu` (the shared CPU budget full), `unsupported` (a rate under 48 kS/s or an offset out of the window), `start-failed`, `encrypted`, `record-disabled` (no covering job records analog), `unclean` (edge, spur, image, truncated, too wide or unverified tuning; the decision's `unclean` names which), `store` (the store refused admission), `close-only` (classed `analog-fm` only by the snapshot that closed the track, while no recording ran for it: nothing is left to record). `slots`, `cpu`, `not-analog`, `tuning`, `no-iq` and `channel-owned` are retried on the next snapshot. `encrypted`, `record-disabled` and `unclean` on a snapshot that has no recording yet are retried too: a later snapshot that is clear, opted in or clean may still start one; the same refusal of a recording in progress discards it and ends the observation's recording. `store` is retried once: the observation's next snapshot may start a new recording, and a second store refusal ends it (a store failure of a file that already finished or waits for its slot refuses that file only). `unsupported` and `start-failed` end the observation's recording. Discards: `not-analog` (a definite other class before a snapshot classed it `analog-fm`, including after a class by its own audio), `unclassed` (closed without an `analog-fm` class, a digital-class recording not classed within 12 s, or a file of a recording classed only by its own audio that fails that class over its whole length), `no-discovery`, `unvoiced` (8 s in the track without voice, 3 s for a digital-class recording, or a finished file that never sounded like voice), `too-short` (under 200 ms in the track), `source-lost` (the IQ ring overran before the file sounded like voice), `empty`, `buffer-full` (60 s held in memory), `store-backlog`, `write-failed`, `park-expired` (a retune-ended shadow whose close never came), `aborted` (the store aborted the file) and `error`. Each decision about a recording (its start, class, voice, eviction, final decision, or the refusal or discard that ended it) is logged at info (`Analog recorder decision`, with the code, the source and the core's observation and discovery ids), at most 20 lines a minute: a recording costs 3 or 4 lines, so about five recordings a minute are logged in full and the log stays under 28 800 lines a day; past it the lines are counted and the next logged line carries `suppressed`. A snapshot refused before any recording started for it (`not-analog`, `tuning`, `slots`, `cpu` and the other snapshot refusals) is logged at debug only (`Analog recorder snapshot refusal`), outside that limit, so a busy band's refusals never crowd a recording's decisions out of the info log. A refusal that repeats the observation's last decision (a snapshot retried while the slots stay full) is not a new decision, but it still attaches the discovery the snapshot now carries; an association first given by the closing snapshot is attached too. Every recording that reached `started` or `shadow` ends with exactly one final decision per file: `finalized`, `evicted`, a `refused:` or a `discarded:` code (one refusal or discard of a whole recording ends all its files at once; a file whose finish was already asked for ends with its own `finalized` or `discarded:aborted`). `GET /api/scanner` reports `recorder`: the live sessions (`shadow`, `classed` or `voiced`, whether a file is being written, and how far the demodulator is behind the air in ms), parked sessions, the last 64 decisions about recordings (`recent`, about the last 12 to 20 recordings at 3 to 5 decisions each), the last 32 snapshot refusals apart from them (`recentRefusals`, so refusals never push a recording's decisions out), counts per code and the suppressed log lines. `GET /api/scanner/discoveries/:id` reports `recorder` too: the newest decision about the discovery's observations and counts per code (the recorder remembers its last 256 observations).
- **With the channel tier.** When the channel-tier recorder owns an observation, this recorder stands down for it, and the channel tier does not start on an observation this recorder owns.
- **Not recorded yet.** AM signals (airband) are not recorded: the recorder only demodulates NFM. Sources whose IQ is not 8-bit unsigned (`U8_IQ`; for example SDR++ sources sending 16-bit or float samples) record nothing: the recorder ignores every snapshot from them. Sources under 48 kS/s (the demodulation rate) record nothing either: such a track reserves no CPU and is not tried again. Both are planned follow-ups.

Store CPU: a wide sweep no longer saturates the main thread in the discovery store. Sticky association searches a per-source, per-family index ordered by each discovery's cached median centre (recomputed only when its observations change) instead of scanning every discovery; per-job counts by confidence are kept incrementally; change detection uses revision counters instead of whole-record clones and string compares; and the `discoveries.json` snapshot is written by a worker thread from a mirror fed by the journal, with the old main-thread path as the fallback (the same bytes either way). Measured at 10 000 discoveries and 50 000 observations: 41.5 µs per ingest, 1.1 µs per progress count build and 1.71 ms of main-thread time per steady snapshot; the first snapshot after boot, a mirror reset or a worker restart still runs on the main thread (about 1.2 s at that size). At startup the snapshot is validated and the store restored in slices of 1,024 discoveries or observations with an event-loop turn between them, so other services (the Activity store open among them) keep running during a large load; at 3,840 discoveries and 76,800 observations the longest main-thread block of the load fell from 1.7 s to 0.65 s (the one-piece JSON parse). The worker heap limit scales with host memory (256 to 2048 MB); a host under about 6 GB writes very large snapshots on the main thread. A snapshot text above V8's maximum string length (about 512 MiB, roughly 10 000 discoveries with 256 observations each) cannot be built on either path.

Merges and evictions: when a merge drops a discovery into a survivor, the dropped discovery's folders stay in place and become the survivor's aliases (persisted, at most 64 per discovery; past that the oldest becomes an orphan, warned as a count). Retention, `recordingPath`, Keep and removal treat alias folders as the survivor's, and removing the survivor removes them too, file by file through the keep decision. A `maxDiscoveries` eviction removes the evicted discovery's folders like an age prune and sends `scanner:discovery` `deleted`; a discovery holding a kept recording is never evicted. A scanner digital voice recording can be deleted with `DELETE /api/recordings/:id` in protected mode (see [API.md](API.md#put-and-delete-apirecordingsidkeep-delete-apirecordingsid)).

### Ignore skips sweeps

Marking a discovery Ignore (`PATCH /api/scanner/discoveries/:id` with `lockout: true`) stops its probes, blocks listen and takes its frequency out of sweeps on the sources it was heard on. Each lockout becomes a runtime exclusion of width max(the discovery's bandwidth, the narrowest raster step of the ranges holding it, 1 kHz) centred on it and clipped to the job's enabled ranges. The sweep's hops no longer list that channel; a hop whose channels are all locked out is dropped. In-window jobs are unchanged. Rules: at most 256 lockout exclusions apply per job (lowest frequency first, the rest are listed unapplied); lockouts never empty a plan (if they would, none applies and all are listed unapplied); they are never written into the job's spec; while a lockout stays set, a PATCH or a restart plans with it again. Clearing or deleting the lockout restores the coverage. A locked-out discovery is never evicted at `maxDiscoveries` or pruned by `discoveryMaxAgeDays` (sweeps no longer hear it, so its last-seen time stops moving); delete it to drop it. Setting, clearing or deleting a lockout replans the affected sweep jobs at once. The plan preview and the job show them as `lockoutExclusions` with `applied`.

### Hit statistics

Sweep job progress exposes per-hop visit, time and hold totals (see [API](API.md)).

Each job counts its hits: one per observation attributed to a discovery, per discovery with first and last hit time and whether the job first saw it. `GET /api/scanner/jobs/:id/hits` lists them (most recent first); the job's `progress.hitStats`, `scanner:progress` (`hits`, `newDiscoveries`) and `scanner:discovery` (`jobHits`) carry them too. In memory only: reset when a job starts (not on resume), dropped with the job, empty after a restart. Bounds: 2,000 discoveries tracked per job (later ones count in the totals only) and a 4,096-observation dedupe memory; past either bound the stats say `truncated: true` and are best-effort.

## Listening

`POST /api/scanner/discoveries/:id/listen` retunes live audio's `offsetHz` to the discovery when it is in the current window (409 `OUT_OF_WINDOW` otherwise). Probe audio is on the digital voice server at `/decoders/<probeId>/stream`.

**Identify** (`POST /api/scanner/identify`, spec § 10.8): click a signal on the waterfall and every plausible decoder tries it, in the current window and never retuning (409 `OUT_OF_WINDOW` outside it; 409 `SCANNER_SOURCE_BUSY` while a sweep holds the tuner). A click on the DC spike, a spur or comb tooth, or an IQ image is answered at once with `artefact` and no trial; broadband RFI is the answer only when nothing transmits within `timeoutMs` (15 s). Identify's trials run one at a time as offset probes with 300 ms pre-roll, each for its hold, and preempt passive and job probes; the channel path that would run them in parallel is not wired into identify yet. A decode awaiting its pair keeps its run 3 080 ms (the 2 s separation that "Decodes reach the discovery" above requires, plus three DMR superframes), once per run, so one continuous transmission confirms within one request; a run with no decode keeps its hold. The result (`identified`, `candidate` or `unidentified`) always carries the measurements and the per-protocol trial list, and the observation lands in the discovery store like any other. Each final target also carries a closed `verdict` ([API.md](API.md#post-apiscanneridentify)). A request a running job already answers returns that job (200, not 201), as long as the job can still listen for at least 90 % of what the request asks. A `decoded` whose match came before the job carries `confirmedAcrossJobs: true`. The two 409s carry `details.next`, the tune that clears them. If the window moves off the target, the result carries `ended: "window-moved"` and open trials are `cancelled`. Identify jobs cannot be paused or edited and are not kept across restarts; pausing the receiver feed ends one (starting or running) as `cancelled` with reason `source-paused` and the results so far.

**Identify for analog voice.** There is no probe decoder for analog FM or AM: the `analog-fm` and `analog-am` trials read the live demodulator's squelch-delimited transmissions (decoder `live-audio`, the latest 32 kept) on the job's source within the target's tolerance, back to 30 s before the job started. A CTCSS or DCS tone on a `commanded` or `relay` frequency makes the target `identified` (`classified`), anything else that matches `candidate`; the tone is only ever the one the transmission tracker reported. Identify never starts or retunes live audio, so the trials end `unsupported`/`no-live-audio` when live audio was not on the target for the whole window, and `no-sync` when it was but nothing keyed up. A digital decode keeps priority. Probe decoders whose program cannot run are listed in `result.decodersUnavailable` and are not respawned for 300 s. Details in [API.md](API.md#post-apiscanneridentify).

**Heard versus decoded.** An analog trial ends `decoded` only when the matching transmission carried a CTCSS tone or a DCS code; a squelch opening without either ends `heard` (it was heard, nothing was decoded). The verdict rule is unchanged: `identified` still needs a tone or code on a `commanded` or `relay` frequency. A live-audio analog match does not stop the digital trials (a successful analog replay does stop the remaining replay trials): it ends only the other analog trials, and every probe and ambient trial runs to its own end, so a digital signal heard through live audio still gets its digital decoders (an identify job with analog evidence can take until its digital trials end).

**Narrow digital trials.** When the target lies in a band-plan range with a channel raster of 6.25 kHz or less (PMR446, land mobile), identify also tries `nxdn48`, `dpmr` and `dstar` even when the track's width suggests 12.5 kHz, scored at least 0.3 before the band priors (a prior of 0 still removes them). For an analog-looking track at 446 MHz the last narrow run ends about 10.6 s in, within the default 15 s.

**Known protocol first.** A discovery that decoded keeps its protocol as the known protocol: identify (and replay, below) try that decoder first, then every other plausible trial in the usual order, so nothing is skipped when the known one fails. The memory decays safely: a known trial that ran its full hold on a transmission and decoded nothing counts a miss, any accepted decode resets the count, and after 2 consecutive misses the discovery is planned like an undecoded signal until it decodes again. Misses are kept in memory only and never change the stored protocol or confidence. Scan job probes count misses the same way, except sweep probes (a hop cut cannot be told from an expired hold) and a protocol only a probe's last mode covers (`nxdn96`), which only identify can decay.

## IQ capture on detection

On by default in protected access mode (`scanner.iqCapture.enabled: true`; in legacy mode it is not started and one info line says so). It exists so a transmission can be identified after it ended, when no decoder was running in time. The flow is fixed: **capture → queue → replay trials → result → discard**.

1. **Capture.** A native tool (`wavekit-iqcap`, shipped next to `wavekit-chan`) keeps a 2 s full-rate ring of the source's IQ from the spectrum engine's tap. When a track surfaces and its discovery has no confirmed result for this transmission (not decoded yet, or a known protocol not yet confirmed live on it), and it is not a spur or an image, the scanner asks for a capture: the channel shifted and decimated to 48 kHz complex float32 (96 kHz for an ISM band or an OOK burst, or 192 kHz for a LoRa candidate, see below), from the pre-roll (500 ms by default) to the track's close or 4 s (for a track classed `analog-fm`, 2 s after the track started: see Early analog classification). At most 6 per source per minute and 2 at once; at most 256 MiB on disk. A track that passed the gate but has no discovery yet is captured too, so its first transmission is not lost: such captures take at most 2 of the 6 per minute. A pending capture whose signal becomes a discovery afterwards gives its share back at once, so a carrier admitted a few seconds after its gate pass does not block the next minute's short bursts. A short burst (a data packet, a push-to-talk over) that only becomes a discovery when it ends is captured at that moment: the ring still holds a burst that ended within its 2 s.
2. **Queue.** A finished capture joins one first-in, first-out replay queue. An identify job near it takes it over instead (at most 2, newest first) and replays it after its live runs.
3. **Replay trials.** Up to 8 trials: the known protocol first, then the probe modes the captured track scores (narrow digital included). They run one at a time through the `replay` probe transport (dsd-fme, multimon-ng, direwolf, rtl_433, acarsdec, dumpvdl2 and Meshtastic LoRa), faster than real time, at 0.15 cores of the probe budget, below identify and above passive. They do not need a live spectrum engine, so the queue drains even after the passive guard closed the engine.
4. **Result.** The first accepted decode is the result and ends the remaining trials; otherwise "no decode". A replay decode is accepted by the same rule as a live probe, except ACARS and VDL2: one CRC-clean acarsdec block or dumpvdl2 frame is enough on a replay, where a live decode needs two (see below). The result lands on the capture's observation like a live decode and feeds the known-protocol memory. An ACARS or VDL2 result whose observation has no discovery of its own lands at its channel the way a live ACARS or VDL2 decode does: it proves the one stored discovery within 4 kHz of the channel, else the channel's decoded record (opened if needed), and never opens a second record beside it. A live observation whose decode the channel's record took stays with that record: its later updates and its close open no second record at the channel. The same holds for a live ACARS or VDL2 decode placed on an observation without a discovery. The proof then says `replay`; it needs the capture's tuning to be `commanded` or `relay`.

5. **Discard.** The capture is deleted as soon as its result exists, or unreplayed when a live decode came first. One that never got a result is deleted 10 min after it finished. A capture across an epoch change, with a bad file or a failed process is never replayed. Nothing survives a restart; the startup sweep removes only files that are provably captures.

**Pagers come first in paging ranges.** In the EU the band plans include the French paging band (466.025 to 466.230 MHz on 12.5 kHz channels, plus the single pager channel at 446.5325 MHz), and the scanner also treats the wider 466 MHz paging range (465.975 to 466.550 MHz) as pager territory. A signal there that looks like 2-level FSK, narrow 4-level FSK or narrow FM is tried with the POCSAG/FLEX decoder before DMR and before the analog trial, so an analog "heard" never skips the pager decoder, even when the analog trial worked on that signal before. A 2-tone FSK signal with about 9 kHz between its tones (POCSAG and FLEX at 1200 or 2400 baud) is treated as a pager signal, never as digital voice.

**Data bursts try their data decoders first.** A bursty data emission is a signal with two FSK tones (two near-equal humps at least 6 kHz apart with a dip between them: POCSAG, FLEX, an FSK sensor), an OOK burst, or, in a band plan range that lists ISM, a burst of at most 1 s or one keyed on at most 75 % of the time (TDMA digital voice excepted). Its replay plan runs rtl_433 first when the band lists ISM, then POCSAG/FLEX when it has two tones, then the other probes, and the analog trial last. Sustained FM voice keeps the analog trial first. A two-tone signal gets the class `fsk2-wide`, not `analog-fm`.

**One emission, one track.** When the dip between the two tones of an FSK signal reaches the noise floor (a POCSAG pager at about 10 dB, an ISM sensor with ±30 kHz deviation), the tones show as two pieces. Pieces that key up on the same 50 ms block (or one block apart), are within 6 dB of each other, about as wide as each other (at most twice), and closer than one channel (0.8 of the channel raster, 10 kHz on a 12.5 kHz raster) or, in an ISM range without a raster, 150 kHz, are joined into one track for as long as it lasts. The pieces of a 125 kHz LoRa chirp join the same way. Two independent signals only join when they key up within 100 ms of each other with matching level and width; nothing joins on the first block after a retune.

**The scanner remembers what worked.** Every replay trial's outcome is kept per discovery (which decoder, how it ended, when, how often it ran) and survives a restart (`trial-memory.json` in the scanner storage directory; a damaged file is ignored and the memory starts empty). The next capture of that signal is tried in this order: decoders that decoded it before, the known protocol, decoders that ran without failing, untried decoders, then decoders that failed. A decoder that failed k times in a row waits 1, 2, 4 … minutes (at most 1 hour) before it is tried again, unless nothing else is left. At most 16 decoders per discovery and 2 048 discoveries are remembered; the oldest go first. The memory is not shown in the API yet.

**More decoders, when they can replay.** The replay plan also names rtl_433 for ISM signals (a band plan that lists ISM at the capture, an OOK burst, or a known ISM discovery, whose trial then runs first), acarsdec for ACARS (a known ACARS discovery's trial runs first), dumpvdl2 for VDL2, AIS-catcher for AIS, the LoRa decoder for LoRa and readsb at 1090 MHz. rtl_433 reads the capture as it is, with every built-in protocol, and a sensor that repeats its frame in one burst is identified from that one capture (two matching checksummed decodes). 48 kHz is not enough for every OOK sensor: rtl_433 decodes 500 µs pulses at 48 kHz, but merges pulses of about 220 µs and decodes nothing (a recorded weather sensor with 229 µs pulses and a synthetic 220/408 µs frame both decode at 96 and 192 kHz and not at 48 kHz). So a capture where the band plan lists ISM, or of an OOK burst, is made at 96 kHz with the same 47.4 kHz passband; audio decoders and the analog trial get it brought down to 48 kHz. FSK sensors whose deviation reaches past ±23.7 kHz do not fit a 48/96 kHz capture’s passband and need a wider capture or live input. The native replay tool can resample VDL2 to exactly 105 kHz (oversample 1), retaining its 25 kHz channel; dumpvdl2 then consumes U8 IQ. Detection and identify captures whose candidate frequency lies in the EU LoRa sub-bands defined in `src/core/iq-capture/rates.ts` (867–868.6 MHz and 869.4–869.65 MHz, both endpoints included) request 192 kHz unless the LoRa prior is explicitly 0. Missing or non-finite candidate frequencies do not raise the rate. Other captures request 192 kHz when their band plan lists LoRa with a prior of at least 1. LoRa captures use 192 kHz with a 150 kHz passband (125 kHz plus a 12.5 kHz guard on each side; scaled from the default passband and capped to keep the capture filter cheap), and replay a 125 kHz channel at 250 kHz (two samples per chip) into the U8 Python wrapper. The replay trial uses LongModerate (SF11, coding rate 4/8). The 250 kHz-bandwidth presets ShortFast, ShortSlow, MediumFast, MediumSlow and LongFast cannot be replayed: the maximum capture rate is 192 kHz, which cannot retain a 250 kHz channel. Resampling to 250 kHz does not restore the missing bandwidth. Older capture tools must explicitly acknowledge the wider passband before 192 kHz captures are requested; otherwise capture falls back to 48 kHz, which cannot replay LoRa. The planner reports `lora-needs-192khz` in its skipped trials for any capture whose rate is not exactly 192 kHz (including legacy captures without a rate), so these trials consume no queue slot. Automatic VDL2 and LoRa replay trials run through the probe pool, using the same CRC-clean confirmation rules as their live decoders. AIS-catcher and readsb also remain live-only. acarsdec does replay: the capture filtered to the ACARS channel (9.2 kHz, see below), AM-demodulated at 48 kHz and resampled to 12 kHz audio. A signal's measured centre can sit a little off its assigned channel. ACARS and VDL2 channels all lie on the 25 kHz raster, so a detection or identify capture of a signal less than 4 kHz from a raster channel between 117.975 and 137 MHz is made centred on that channel, and decoded and named there (an 8.33 kHz voice channel, 8.3 kHz off the raster, keeps its own centre). That filter is the replay tool's own (`--bandwidth 9200 --transition 1000`), and acarsdec adds none on replay: it is flat over an ACARS block (±3.6 kHz) plus 1 kHz of carrier tolerance (aircraft transmitter 5 ppm, receiver 1 ppm and Doppler at 137 MHz), about 3 dB down at 5 kHz, 6 dB at 5.1 kHz, and at least 65 dB down from 5.6 kHz, so an 8.33 kHz voice neighbour (85 dB down) and every 25 kHz neighbour are stopped. A carrier about 5 kHz off the channel is only about 3 dB down and is not removed. Live acarsdec keeps its 25 kHz channel unless its experimental `channelFilter` option is on (see the decoder guide). dumpvdl2 gets the capture centre as `--centerfreq` and the channel as the frequency it decodes, at most 4.1 kHz apart: 12.5 kHz of passband minus the 8.4 kHz half-width of a VDL2 burst. A replay pairs identities only within itself, and a capture holds at most 4 s. So ONE ACARS block with acarsdec `error` 0 (valid framing, parity and CRC-16, no bit corrected) is the decode of that replay, named by its registration. So is ONE VDL2 frame that dumpvdl2 printed: it prints a frame only after the burst header CRC, the Reed-Solomon FEC and the frame's CRC-16 have passed. The frame is named by its source address, which must not be 000000 or FFFFFF, and an embedded ACARS block that failed its CRC disqualifies it. Live and configured acarsdec and dumpvdl2 decodes keep the two-decode rule.

**Decode a stored snapshot offline.** `scripts/decode-snapshot.ts` runs this same chain on a CU8 snapshot (`POST /api/sources/:sourceId/iq-snapshot`, at most 10 s): `wavekit-iqcap run` cuts a 48 kHz capture per channel and 4 s window, `wavekit-iqcap replay` filters it, and acarsdec or dumpvdl2 runs with the replay configuration. It prints every decoder line with the scanner's verdict (CRC-clean or not, identity) and a result per channel. Channels must be 25 kHz ACARS or VDL2 channels within rate / 2 − 23.7 kHz of the snapshot centre (±488.3 kHz at 1.024 Msps). Build it with `node scripts/build-file.mjs scripts/decode-snapshot.ts`, then run `node dist/decode-snapshot.js --file /abs/iq0.cu8 --rate 1024000 --centre 133600000 --channel 133550000 --decoder acarsdec` where `wavekit-iqcap` and the decoder are on `PATH` (the core image). `--plan-only` prints the windows, offsets and tool arguments without running anything. Results print only once each decoder's output is complete. It exits 1 when a step fails (a tool or the decoder exits nonzero or is killed by a signal, or a pipe to one of them fails before the step finished), 2 on bad arguments and 3 when a program is missing. A pipe reset (EPIPE or ECONNRESET) after a step finished is normal: a tool that exits after its last command leaves that line unread.

**The first transmission is captured too.** A signal that passed the channel gate gets a capture even before it has become a discovery, at most 2 per source per minute (leaving at least 4 of the 6 for discoveries). When its replay decodes, the signal becomes a discovery with that decode. A replay that ends after the signal has left the scanner's short recent list (about 10 s) still lands when exactly one stored discovery of that source sits within 6.25 kHz (half a 12.5 kHz channel) of the captured frequency and can take that decode; with none, two or more, or one already decoded as another protocol, it is dropped.

**A live decoder does not skip the capture yet.** Every signal that passed the channel gate is captured within the limits above, even on a channel a paging or digital-voice decoder already listens to: the scanner cannot yet tell exactly when a signal started relative to when that decoder started (it would need the time each sample arrived), so it never assumes the decoder heard the start.

**Capture size.** A capture is complex float32: 8 bytes per sample, so a full 4 s capture is 48 000 × 4 × 8 = 1 536 000 bytes (about 1.5 MB) at 48 kHz and 3 072 000 bytes at 96 kHz or 6 144 000 bytes at 192 kHz. The 256 MiB cap holds about 174 full 48 kHz captures, 87 at 96 kHz or 43 at 192 kHz; a capture that would pass it is refused. At 6 captures per source per minute that is at most about 9.2 MB per source per minute at 48 kHz and 18.4 MB if every one is at 96 kHz, or 36.9 MB at 192 kHz.

**Decodes from busy-channel decoders land on their channel.** A paging or digital-voice decoder that the planner moved to a band-plan channel reports its decodes at that channel, so they land on that channel's discovery (not at the tuned centre). Each such channel confirms its own identities: a colour code or capcode heard on one channel never confirms a decode on another.

dsd-fme excludes a channel while the ranker has a decode of a non-digital-voice
protocol within its existing 15-minute decode-hot window (strictly younger than
900,000 ms). DMR, P25, NXDN, YSF, dPMR and D-Star remain voice candidates;
paging, APRS, ACARS and other protocols do not. This evidence travels through the
activity snapshot, including decode history pulled by the ranker. Association
requires `|candidate centre − decoded channel centre| ≤ channelWidthHz / 4`:
3,125 Hz for a 12,500 Hz channel, including that boundary and narrower than half
a channel. Ambiguous associations exclude neither candidate. A recent checked
voice decode on that same centre wins over non-voice evidence for the same
900,000 ms window, regardless of which decode came later. Recent verified sync
on the exact running placement also overrides exclusion while its protection lasts.
Thus a pager at H + 6,250 Hz cannot suppress DMR at H.

A running dsd-fme activity slot without clean checked sync after its full dwell is
demoted for `max(60,000, dwellMs × max(1, maxMovesPerMinute))` milliseconds:
with the defaults, `max(60,000, 20,000 × 6) = 120,000 ms`. This allows six other
full dwells before retrying a failed channel. A zero move budget still holds
for a full 60,000 ms window (or one dwell, if longer). The cooldown applies strictly
before its deadline; at or after it a fresh run on the channel may compete again.
An incumbent without recent clean sync stays demoted until clean sync or a move,
even after cooldown expiry; continuous energy cannot revive that failed run when
a challenger arrives late. Demoted channels cannot challenge or fill free slots;
replacing a demoted incumbent still spends the shared per-source move budget.
There are at most 256 demotions (one quarter of the 1,024-candidate query cap),
oldest insertion dropped first.

Only explicitly `verified: true`, `crcErr: false` lines with absent or zero
`fecErrs` clear demotion and renew protection for the exact running placement.
Protection lasts strictly until `lastCleanSyncMonoMs + cooldownMs` (120 seconds
with defaults), and expires at that deadline even without new spectrum events.
Repeated clean evidence renews it; a restart resets it. Weak sync/CC hints and
CRC/FEC failures never renew protection: their only protection is the run's
initial `dwellMs` (20 seconds by default), expiring at or after the dwell boundary.

**Busy channels get the decoders.** Paging (POCSAG/FLEX) and digital-voice (DMR and similar) decoders can only run on a few channels of a band at once. The scanner ranks every channel those decoders could use by recent activity, and the decoder planner puts its spare channel slots on the busiest ones instead of only the ones nearest the tuned centre:

- a channel with signal energy in the latest spectrum block (no older than 1 s) always ranks first; an overloaded block shows no energy, and 10 s of overloaded blocks count as no fresh spectrum;
- energy in the last 2 minutes, or an accepted decode (timed by when it was decoded, not by later activity) or successful replay in the last 15 minutes, makes it hot; energy or a gated signal in the last 15 minutes, or a decode in the last 6 hours, makes it warm; nothing newer makes it cold again, so no channel stays busy forever;
- long-term occupancy only nudges the order, it never makes a channel busy on its own;
- a replay that ran on the whole capture and decoded nothing pushes the channel down for a minute; a replay that was cut short, cancelled or skipped for CPU counts for nothing;
- a signal is credited to the nearest channel within half a channel width; a signal halfway between two channels is credited to neither.

When the spectrum has no fresh block for 10 s, while the receiver retunes, or when it stops, the ranking is not used and the planner falls back to the nearest-first order. Updates reach the planner at most 250 ms late. The ranking costs about 2 µs of main-thread time per spectrum block on the reference Mac.

Cost on the reference Mac: about 0.06 to 0.14 cores for the tool with 2 captures active (0.0002 with the ring only), about 11.4 MB of memory per source outside the file cap, and no measurable event-loop cost for the tap feed. Details and bounds in [API.md](API.md#iq-capture-on-detection).

## Honesty and limits

- Detection is measured on one strong DMR capture; false-alarm control adapts per range (CFAR), but a fresh dongle needs minutes to learn its spurs.
- A sweep can miss short transmissions: the preview reports probability of intercept per hop for `txDurationHintMs`, with each hop's own dwell. A broad sweep is mostly settle time: 172 hops with a 1 s timer settle take about 198 s, so a 30 s transmission on a quiet hop is caught about 15 % of the time and a 5 s one about 2.5 %; hops with recent activity get the revisit slots and much better odds.
- `scanner:progress` reaches WS clients at up to 2 Hz per job (one per 500 ms); every other scanner message keeps one per key per second, and all share the `ws.maxMessagesPerSecond` budget.
- That budget is shared with `scanner:activity`: when slots are scarce the limiter sends state messages first, then progress, then activity, so faster progress can delay activity updates (each still gets its latest state eventually).
- P25, NXDN, D-STAR, dPMR and DMR repeater rules are unvalidated until captures exist; those results stay `candidate` unless dsd-fme decodes them.
- Marker settle needs the RTL2832 test mode to reach the core; over some rtlmux/Wi-Fi paths it does not, and timer settle makes sweeps about 6× slower.
- Before the channelizer, one confirm probe runs at a time; on a Pi 4 even that may exceed the CPU budget.

### Quality benchmark

`scripts/bench/scanner-quality.ts` measures whether what shows on the waterfall is found and identified, and how fast. A small scene (1.024 Msps, 12 s, one signal per class) runs in CI (`tests/unit/core/scanner/scanner-quality-bench.test.ts`). SNR is carrier power over the noise in max(12.5 kHz, the signal's width).

### Why a DMR band scan found only Heard signals

A sweep decides whether to probe a track at the end of its hop dwell, about 150 ms (3 blocks) after the settle. A track that young carries no TDMA signature yet: the envelope autocorrelation needs at least 300 ms and the first track update that has it comes after 1 s. The spectral classifier labels a track of up to 4 blocks an OOK burst unless the band's ISM prior is below 1, and in a sweep the classifier saw no band prior when no job range at that frequency named protocols (a 135 to 174 MHz range is enclosed by no single built-in plan). So every young land-mobile track became an ISM burst, which has no probe mode: no probe, no deferral, no decode, and the discovery stayed `activity` or `candidate`. Identify was not affected because it uses the band-plan priors. Fixed: a sweep over a range that names no protocols takes the band plan's ISM prior at that frequency, except inside the OOK short-range-device allocations (314 to 316, 433.05 to 434.79, 863 to 870 and 902 to 928 MHz), where a short burst stays an OOK burst. A second cause: the dsd-fme `auto` (DMR) mode held for 600 ms, below a DMR late entry (about 1.8 s); its hold is now 1 800 ms (which also lengthens P25 and other `auto` probes). Ruled out: hop dwell and the probe budget. Still conditional: the CPU guard defers probes when the container runs above 90 % of its CPU quota, and such deferrals are recorded as `budget`.

### Known limitations

- Relay and live audio default to the first non-dedicated source. Explicit source selection routes that source's IQ, capabilities and controls together. If every source is dedicated, implicit selection is rejected; select a source explicitly. Legacy live-demod callers supplying a fixed fanout cannot switch radios at runtime without supplying the source router.
- A dedicated source added at runtime (`POST /api/sources`) is not excluded from the default-source choice until the core restarts. This matters only when no shared source existed at boot.
- An enabled relay without an explicit source cannot start when there are no configured shared sources (including an empty source list). The core exits during startup; configure a shared source, select a source explicitly, or disable the relay.
- After a fatal spectrum worker failure, passive discovery can remain failed until a source reconnect, capability change or settings update rebuilds it. `passive.enabled` describes the configured job's presence, not successful scanning. Inspect its job state and the spectrum engine error.
- Spectrum-only clients receive no automatic profile notification for that fatal failure. Consult `scanner:status` or `GET /api/spectrum` when frames stop; re-subscribe after cleanup to request a fresh engine. Automatic bounded passive recovery and direct display failure notification remain follow-up work.
- The CLI's `6 Scan` view ignores scanner fields it does not know yet (newer track fields, for example); the web app's Scanner drawer shows them.
- Per-range `sensitivity` changes only the detector's false-alarm target in that range; integration time stays job-wide. Ranges with identical edges share one target (the most sensitive one set; a range without one counts at the detector's current target). Where ranges overlap only partly, the detector uses the first matching range; overlaps are not split.
- Per-range `dwellMs` applies to whole hops: a hop that also covers a neighbouring range dwells as long as the longest of them.

### Rules as implemented (spec wording notes)

These are the rules the code follows where the design spec's wording differs:

- `decoded` is reached by the first accepted identity (the protocol's confirm rule), not by any identity-bearing evidence.
- The spectrum worker emits a block summary for every block except alarm (`untracked-retune`) blocks, so an alarm block produces no `spectrum:frame`.
- Impulse blanking (spec § 4.9, R6): a frame is blanked when it is at least 6 dB over the previous block's integrated mean power per bin on at least 50 % of the usable bins; the first block of an epoch is judged against its own per-bin median ÷ ln 2. Periodic RFI is active after at least 3 impulse events (blanked frames within 2 N samples count as one) whose last two intervals agree within ± 10 %; the period is their mean. (The spec says "over the per-bin floor" and "3 blanked frames"; the floor reference would blank steady wideband occupants.)
- IQ image match (spec § 4.9, R7): the −6 dB OBWs agree within `max(30 % of A, 2 RBW)`. (The spec says "± 30 %"; the 2 RBW term is needed for 1-RBW tones.)

## Spectrum and waterfall

The `spectrum` WS channel carries `spectrum:frame` messages from the core spectrum service: `{sourceId, profile, epoch, tuningTrust, centerHz, sampleRateHz, binHz, nativeBinHz, startHz, binCount, bins, binEncoding: "u8-halfdb-127.5", floorDbfs, thresholdDb, tracks: [{startHz, endHz, flags: {burst, iqImage, edge}}], masks: {dcHz, dcGuardHz, spurRanges}, snapshot?, at}`, max-hold over the frame interval (restarted when the epoch changes, so a frame never mixes two centres during a sweep). Decoding `bins` (base64 → bytes, dBFS = v / 2 − 127.5):

```js
// frame = msg.data of a "spectrum:frame" message
const bytes = Uint8Array.from(atob(frame.bins), c => c.charCodeAt(0))
const dbfs = Array.from(bytes, v => v / 2 - 127.5)
const hzOf = i => frame.startHz + (i + 0.5) * frame.binHz
// draw dbfs[i] at hzOf(i); grey the axis when frame.tuningTrust === "unverified"
// never merge rows across a change of sourceId, epoch, profile, binHz or startHz
```

**Selection.** `{"type":"subscribe","channels":["spectrum"]}` without options gets the default profile (`scanner.ws.spectrumHz` 10 × `scanner.ws.spectrumBins` 512, both runtime-PATCHable) on every eligible source. `"options":{"spectrum":{"sourceId":"rtl","hz":25,"bins":2048}}` selects one source and one profile for that client: `hz` is snapped to 0.5 and clamped to 0.5–25, `bins` rounded and clamped to 128–2048, omitted fields take the default's values. Subscribing again switches without a gap; subscribing without options returns to the default selection; `unsubscribe` or a disconnect releases the demand. Rapid re-subscribes coalesce: only the latest is answered.

**`spectrum:profile`** is sent to that client only, as the ack to a subscribe or as a notice after a permanent source removal or a default-profile change that remapped it: `{selection: "none" | "default" | "source", sourceId, profile: {key, hz, bins} | null, effectiveHz, outcome: "default" | "exact" | "nearest" | "rejected" | "removed", adjusted: ("hz" | "bins" | "profile-limit" | "averaging" | "backfillMs")[], backfillFrames?, removedSourceId?, code?, message?}`. `selection: "none"` means no frames. A `rejected` request (`SPECTRUM_OPTIONS_INVALID`, `SPECTRUM_SOURCE_NOT_FOUND`, `SPECTRUM_SOURCE_NOT_IQ`, `SPECTRUM_SOURCE_REMOVED`, `SPECTRUM_ENGINE_ERROR`) keeps the previous selection.

**Limits.** The server serves the nearest admissible profile and never rejects a request for its size. Above `scanner.ws.spectrumMaxProfiles` (4 distinct non-default profiles over all sources; config-only, 1–16) a request maps to an active profile on that source (preferring one not larger than the request) or to the default, with `outcome: "nearest"` and `adjusted: ["profile-limit"]`. Averaged profiles count against the same limit; an averaged request maps to the nearest profile with the same averaging, else to averaging off (the default always qualifies), adding `"averaging"` to `adjusted` when the averaging changed. `GET /api/spectrum` reports `limits` (also `averaging` 1–32 and `backfill` bounds).

**Averaging and backfill (opt-in, per client).** `options.spectrum.averaging` (1–32, default 1 = off) gives an exponential average over n frames per bin in dBFS after each frame's max-hold (`avg += (frame − avg) / n`, seeded with the first frame, restarted on a new epoch, stream or bin layout). It is part of the profile: the key becomes `<hz>:<bins>:a<n>` and `profile.averaging` is set only when n > 1; clients that never send `averaging` never get an averaged profile. `options.spectrum.backfillMs` (0–10000, default 0 = none) replays recent frames of the current stream and epoch after the ack; the ack's `backfillFrames` says how many follow. Without either option a client gets plain max-hold frames and no backfill.

**Cadence and resolution.** One frame per `ceil(blockHz / hz)` block summaries, with `blockHz = 1000 / integrationMs` (20 at the default): 25 Hz gives 20 Hz, 10 or 15 Hz gives 10 Hz. `effectiveHz` is that cadence while blocks flow and an upper bound across sweep settles and gaps. `binCount ≤ bins` (never upsampled) and `binHz` is the true width; `nativeBinHz` is `fs / fftSize`, so `binHz / nativeBinHz` is the total max-hold factor. The engine's summary budget `scanner.ws.spectrumSummaryBins` (2048, config-only, 512–8192) caps the detail any profile can get; it is not a client setting. Clients get at most min(host bins cap, summary budget) bins: `scanner.ws.spectrumBinsCap` (`auto`, the default when absent, measures the host once at startup; or a fixed 2048, 4096 or 8192) sets the cap and `limits.binsCap` reports it. The default profile keeps 128–2048; detail above 2048 bins also needs `detection.fftSize` ≥ 4096 at 2.048 Msps.

**Ordering and client rules.** A `spectrum` subscriber gets the current state on subscribe (ack, then one snapshot per source of the current epoch, then live frames). The snapshot is not a replay of missed messages. With `backfillMs` the ack is followed by up to 64 `backfill: true` frames (oldest first, at most 512 KiB of message JSON) before the snapshot; a backfill frame has no `snapshot`, its `at` is the arrival of its newest block, and the snapshot's block is not replayed. Backfill covers only the current epoch, so it never spans a retune. A snapshot frame has `snapshot: true` and is the latest block of the current epoch at the client's profile (none while a retune settles). Reset the waterfall on every `spectrum:profile`, except that an `outcome: "removed"` notice with `selection: "default"` drops only `removedSourceId`'s waterfall. Compare epochs for equality only; they restart with the engine. Spectrum messages bypass the scanner limiter and do not count toward `ws.maxMessagesPerSecond`.

Engines run while a job or a `spectrum` client needs them, also with `scanner.enabled: false`. `tuningTrust` on frames: `unverified` frames have an untrusted frequency axis (grey it out). Status warnings (`GET /api/spectrum` `sources[].warnings`): `untracked-retune` (the scene moved without a command the core saw; the shift is reported when it can be estimated) and `host-centre-mismatch` (the receiver reports another centre than the core's caps). `GET /api/spectrum/occupancy` returns hourly duty per 12.5 kHz bucket for the last 7 days.

**Stable tracks.** A frame track the tracker associated with an open track also carries `trackId`, `snrDb` (lifetime mean), `peakDbfs` (lifetime max), `blocks`, `ageMs` (first seen ≈ `at` − `ageMs`), `surfaced` (recomputed every block, not sticky), `flags.spur` and, when the scanner has a discovery for it, `scanner: {discoveryId, protocol, confidence}`. Key tracks by `sourceId` and `trackId`; the id survives the tracker's 120 s rollover. A track grows by at most 260 bytes of JSON. See [API.md](API.md#spectrumframe).

**Cursor tuning:** inside the window `PATCH /api/live-audio/config {"offsetHz": …}`; outside it `POST /api/tuner/:sourceId/frequency {"hz": …}`, which preempts a running sweep (`paused(preempted-operator)`).

## Burst IQ capture (fixtures)

The job option `capture: {enabled, mode: "wideband" | "channel", preMs: 1000, postMs: 500, onlyDecoded: true, maxPerHour: 6}` (defaults shown; `enabled` is required, `preMs` 0–5000, `postMs` 0–10000, `maxPerHour` 1–60) records the IQ of a transmission with pre-roll from the engine's ring. By default only transmissions that decode are kept, at most `maxPerHour` per job. `PATCH /api/scanner/jobs/:id` can change `capture`.

- **Modes:** `wideband` keeps the whole source window at the source rate (a `channelizer-golden` fixture when it decoded with payload fields at ≥ 2.048 Msps); `channel` keeps a 48 kHz cu8 channel centred on the target (`tail-golden`).
- **Files** under `scanner.storage.dir/captures/`: `<id>.cu8` (IQ), `<id>.json` (exactly a fixtures manifest v2 entry: `role`, `decoder`, `decoder_options`, `sha256`, `sample_rate`, `center_hz`, `duration_s`, `expected` payloads from the decode) and `<id>.scanner.json` (the `ScannerCapture` record: job, observation, discovery, epoch, sample range, pre-roll clipping). `storage.maxCapturesMb` (256) caps the folder; the oldest captures go first.
- **REST:** `GET /api/scanner/captures` (newest first), `GET /api/scanner/captures/<id>.cu8` or `<id>.json`, `DELETE /api/scanner/captures/:id`.
- **Promote a capture to a fixture:** copy the `.cu8` into `fixtures/raw/`, append the `.json` entry to `fixtures/manifest.yaml` under `fixtures:`, and run the fixture checks. Never commit the IQ.
- **Privacy:** payloads in `expected` carry identifiers (talkgroups, radio ids, callsigns); entries are `license: "private"` with `fetch.kind: "private"`.

## Troubleshooting

- **`settleMode: "timer"`** in `GET /api/scanner` `engines[]`: test-mode markers do not reach the core (rtlmux or the network path drops them). Sweeps still work, about 6× slower; check `markerSupported` and the measured `settleMs`.
- **`stuckTestMode: true`**: the receiver stayed in RTL2832 test mode. Send any tuner command (for example the same frequency) and check the rtlmux version.
- **`floorOccupied: true`**: a wideband emitter fills the window, so the floor estimate is high and weak signals hide under it. Tune away or narrow the range.
- **`overload: true`**: the ADC clips. Lower the gain, or use `tuner.overload: "reduce-gain"` on sweeps.
- **The whole window jumps up on one band** (often 868 or 915 MHz with a high manual gain): the front end overloads before the ADC clips, so `overload` stays false. Hops without a job gain use the per-band gain table (`tuner.bandGain`, `PUT /api/tuner-band-gain`), which lowers the gain in the ISM bands by default and gives your exact gain back when the scanner returns the tuner; see [CORE-REFERENCE.md, Per-band gain](CORE-REFERENCE.md#per-band-gain).
- **`overloadHint: { reason: "front end may be overloaded at this gain" }`** in `engines[]`: with a manual gain, at least 5 % of spectrum frames (and at least 50) were blanked as broadband impulses over the last ~30 s at this gain. Steady broadband blanking at a fixed manual gain usually means the front end is overloaded, not that the band is noisy. Lower the gain (or set a band gain) and compare; the hint clears once blanking falls back under the threshold. It never shows in AGC or when the gain is unknown.
- **Passive disabled on a slow host**: `engines[].passive.disabledReason` names the cost: `engine-cpu` (the whole engine, passive alone or saturated) or `passive-cpu` (what passive adds to a shared engine). When a waterfall or another job shares the engine, the guard judges only what passive adds over a baseline it measures with passive briefly off (`measuring baseline`: a one-minute pause, at most once per 30 min, not a fault); a shared engine at 95 % of a core or more turns passive off whatever the baseline. Raise `scanner.passive.maxEngineUtilization` in the config if the host can afford it; a shared engine retries passive every 15 min.
- **No probes start**: probes wait for the CPU budget (`cpuBudgetCores`), the CPU guard (`cpuGuardPercent`, see `cpuGuard.percent`) and `maxProbes.offset`; compare `budgets` in `GET /api/scanner`. Discoveries that never got a probe report `undecodedReason: "budget"`. Passive jobs get no offset probes unless `passive.offsetProbes` is on.
- **`disk: "disk-low"`**: free disk is below `storage.minFreeDiskMb`; recordings, evidence and captures stop until space returns.
- **503 `SCANNER_STATE_UNAVAILABLE`**: the scanner could not read its jobs or settings at boot. It runs without them (in-window jobs and the spectrum feed still work) and never overwrites the unread files; fix the files under `storage.dir` and restart.

## Channel-gated discovery and replay

A fitting one-hop sweep parks after settling: it stays tuned and keeps the epoch
and track ids. Sweeps are counted from received samples covering the dwell,
not elapsed wall time; `sweepPeriodMs` is null while parked. Resuming at the same
verified centre, rate and gain skips the tuner write. Multi-hop revisits that
choose the same centre likewise avoid an unnecessary retune.

The channel-matched gate integrates power and floor across the channel width
(from occupied bandwidth, raster, or the default), rather than judging one bin.
Its threshold `kCh` adapts to false alarms per range: runs ending without a pass
count toward adaptation. That adaptation persists across epochs, while slot runs
reset. Defaults require 300 ms at 6 dB channel SNR, or 100 ms at 9 dB for bursts.
A track inside a burst range that other ranges overlap (ISM 433 inside 70 cm and UHF
land mobile) may pass by either rule, so a 150 ms sensor burst passes while a long weak
carrier there still passes by the 300 ms one. A joined FSK track is also measured on its
strongest tone, so the empty gap between its tones does not count against it.
Only passed tracks create discoveries, with one exception: an accepted identity
decode places a short burst even before its gate passes (for example AIS, VDL2
or LoRa), retaining the original observation close.

Labels now come only from evidence. Spectral classes and band priors order trials;
they do not name protocols. Show `proof.summary`, with these forms:

- `DMR decoded (CC1, TG 9)`; decoded identities, talkgroup and encryption are
  included when present.
- `Analog FM voice, CTCSS 88.5` or `Analog FM voice, DCS 023N` (`I` for inverted);
  without voice, omit `voice`; AM uses `Analog AM`.
- `Analog FM voice` or `Analog AM voice` without a tone.
- `Digital, not decoded (tried DMR, dPMR, NXDN)`; unavailable programs are named,
  for example `Digital, not decoded (tried dPMR; dsd-fme unavailable)`.
  With none tried: `Digital, not decoded (no decoder available)`.
- `Carrier only`.

Proofs belong to individual transmissions. A discovery selects its strongest
proof, newest on ties: decoded, analog tone, analog voice, digital undecoded,
carrier only. Summaries are at most 160 characters, abbreviating long trial lists
with `+<n> more`. `evidence=proven` lists proofs; `weak` lists unproven activity;
`all` is the default. Trial, analog and unavailable evidence is in memory only.
After restart, only persisted decode evidence rebuilds proofs; other discoveries
read `weak` until new evidence arrives.

A weak discovery (no proof) reads `unknown` / `activity`, whatever an older
guess stored on the record said; the stored fields are kept. It may carry a
display-only `hint`: `lora` (a short 100 to 150 kHz burst inside an EU LoRa
slot), `ism-device` (a short or OOK burst up to 25 kHz wide in an ISM band; try
rtl_433), `analog-voice` (a longer signal within 3 kHz of the LPD433 25 kHz
raster, 433.075 to 434.775 MHz), otherwise its spectral class. A hint is never
a protocol label. Band-plan priors come from the narrowest covering range that
lists protocols. Inside the EU `ism-433` band the scanner also treats
433.075–434.775 MHz (LPD433, 25 kHz channels, analog FM and ISM) as a narrower
range for trial order and hints, so there analog FM is tried before DMR; it is
not a separate band-plan entry.

Capture starts at gate pass. Scans replay captured IQ before taking a live hold.
Every planned trial is recorded as `pending` first, so skipped or unavailable
work remains visible. A known protocol runs first; otherwise one analog trial
precedes digital probes. Qualified CTCSS or DCS produces `decoded`; voice without
a tone is `heard`. FM requires at least 8 dB discriminator quieting in at least
half the frames; AM requires at least 6 dB quieting. Data clustering is checked
before voice. Outcomes also include `data-like`, `carrier-only` and `no-sync`.
An analog decode or heard voice ends that capture's remaining digital replay;
data-like and carrier-only outcomes still allow digital trials. GMSK can currently
read carrier-only; real-corpus calibration remains open.

Replay and identify share one analog admission slot and the probe pool's core
budget, reserving 0.05 core per analog trial. Measured analyze cost was 14–22 ms
per capture, with 21–33 ms for the sliced tone pass and about 2 ms event-loop p99:
about 0.03 core total in that benchmark. This is not a live-host
capacity guarantee; a Rust tone pass remains a follow-up.

“Decode here” uses `waitUntilSignal: true` with optional `waitMs` (default five
minutes, at most ten). A gate-passed transmission already on air qualifies;
an older nearest track cannot hide a newer eligible one. The trial timeout starts
at the transmission start. No signal ends honestly with `ended: "no-signal"`.
Progress reports settling, holding, trialing, replaying, capturing or watching,
with the current hold/trial and replay queue depth; see [API details](API.md#scanner-evidence-and-activity).
Capture is on by default, and runs only in protected
access mode (`scanner.iqCapture.allowLegacy` is reserved for legacy mode later).

## Identification from running decoders

Configured, running decoders identify scanner discoveries without starting a
probe: readsb (ADS-B), acarsdec (ACARS), dumpvdl2 (VDL2), AIS-catcher (AIS),
rtl_433 (ISM), direwolf and multimon-ng AFSK1200 (APRS), multimon-ng
(POCSAG/FLEX), dsd-fme (digital voice), and Meshtastic LoRa. Their output must
come from the discovery's source with commanded or relay tuning trust. ACARS,
VDL2 and LoRa output with a missing or zero frequency uses the actual channel
placement (tuner centre plus channelizer offset), or the single configured target
when unplaced. Multiple configured targets without a decoder frequency give no
placement; a configured target is never a band default. Ambient evidence from
every decoder also needs trusted tuning: it stops after an external SDR++ retune
until the tuning is trusted again.
Malformed or out-of-channel frequencies prove nothing.

An ambient identity needs two checksum-valid decodes at least two seconds apart
and no more than five minutes apart (ADS-B is the exception: two CRC-valid Mode S
frames confirm with no minimum spacing; aircraft records need advancing message
evidence 2 s to 5 min apart). When the DTO includes content, their logical
message identities must also differ. Reception level, noise, RSSI/SNR, errors,
frequency, channel and timestamps never create a new message. ACARS uses aircraft
registration/flight plus the three-character message number (acarsdec's trailing
block letter is removed; dumpvdl2 already separates `msg_num` and `msg_num_seq`),
so different blocks of one message count
once (without a message number, aircraft, label and text are used). VDL2 uses source
and destination addresses plus embedded ACARS identity, or raw message text;
non-ACARS frames without text use spacing alone. rtl_433 uses model/device ID and
decoded sensor fields; AIS uses vessel content; Meshtastic uses packet ID and
payload; paging with message content uses protocol, capcode, function and text;
empty or tone-only pages use spacing alone. Direwolf APRS uses source, destination
and the information field, excluding digipeater paths and other routing fields.
DSD uses protocol and
link fields (colour/access code, talkgroup, source, slot or callsign). Checked
DMR control bursts use spacing alone, including identical CC/slot repeats; voice
LC and other content-bearing evidence still require distinct content. Address-only
APRS output from multimon and
other DTOs without content use spacing alone. ADS-B retains
its local CRC/address checks and raw-frame duplicate rejection for Beast output.
For SBS output, the adapter emits one aircraft record per `MSG` line: `messageCount: 1`
is a per-line count, not a single-message aircraft snapshot. Confirmation relies on
readsb's own decoding and two independent messages of the same six-digit ICAO,
at least 2 s and at most 5 min apart in readsb's own time, with different full-line
content identities. The adapter carries the generated date/time (SBS fields 7–8),
or logged date/time (9–10) only when both generated fields are absent. Calendar and
clock values are validated; missing or malformed source time cannot confirm.
SBS has no timezone; the adapter normalizes its wall clock to UTC for intervals.
Receipt time and receipt delays never advance SBS confirmation. JSON records carry
`readsbFormat: "json"`; they need a cumulative count greater than one and a later
increasing count, with the same spacing measured in source time (`jsonSourceTimestamp`, derived from numeric
`now` epoch seconds minus nonnegative `seen` age). JSON snapshots without valid
source epoch/age cannot confirm. State is bounded to
4096 addresses per decoder/source epoch.
SBS/JSON inherit readsb's message validation policy. We could not verify offline
the CRC-correction/address-confirmation policy of the pinned readsb build
(`b499ecbd18dc4a2ec6098c31de31508017fa6190`); these records do not prove an
uncorrected CRC or independent address confirmation. The two-message guard above
does not establish those upstream guarantees.
Only configured local decoders with trusted receiver tuning count. For raw readsb
placements without a channel or configured frequencies, its protocol declaration
supplies the 1090 MHz target; the provenance index still requires an observed source
centre/rate covering that target (within 0.45 × sample rate), unchanged since decoder
start. Unknown or merely configured tuning and external-input decoders fail closed. Accepted ambient
identities reset known-protocol misses and record successful trial memory;
untrusted evidence does neither. Edge tracks use these same strict checks.

Point association requires the decode frequency inside the track's occupied
span, widened by a 2.5 kHz placement tolerance: reach = max(1.5 kHz, occupied
bandwidth / 2) + 2.5 kHz, with an exclusive boundary. The tolerance budgets
1 kHz decoder/raster agreement plus 1.5 kHz measured-centre error; 2.5 kHz is
less than half the tightest 12.5 kHz raster (6.25 kHz). Even with the minimum
half-span, reach extends at most 4 kHz beyond the measured half-width. This applies to paging,
APRS, digital voice, 25 kHz AIS/ACARS/VDL2 and wide LoRa alike: channel width
alone cannot attach a decode to a narrow neighbouring signal. A merged wide
track can contain several channels. ADS-B retains its separate nominal-frequency
centre tolerance of 500 kHz, inclusive.
The observation lifetime must include the decode time, with two seconds of
pairing margin on either side.

rtl_433 uses its placed channel or capture span. A usable JSON frequency
estimate narrows matches to 60 kHz plus half the track's occupied bandwidth.
Near the capture edge the estimate is unreliable, so the whole detection span
is used. Activity rows also retain the estimate in Hz: JSON `freq` is MHz,
or `(freq1 + freq2) / 2` for FSK. Values must be finite numbers in the
usable tuner window (centre ± 0.8 × sample rate / 2); malformed or out-of-window
values fall back to the placed target.

When reaching candidates belong to different discoveries, a channel fragment
nested inside every other reaching track span takes precedence. Thus a merged
wide track covering two ACARS channels yields to each channel's own fragment;
without that fragment it receives the decode itself. A fragment on the other
channel does not reach the decode unless its measured occupied span already
covers it. ADS-B instead keeps nearest-wins: the
nearest reaching track is named, and tracks of different discoveries tied
within 1 Hz of that distance receive nothing. Other point multi-discovery
cases receive no decode. Point ambiguity is logged at most once
per decode, after interpretation; shared-confirmation lookups do not log.
Multiple observations of one discovery can receive point evidence. rtl_433
requires exactly one qualifying observation. Ambiguous evidence cannot prime
shared confirmation.

Ambient observation lookups use a per-source sorted frequency index:
O(log n + k) per decode for n indexed observations and k candidates in the
queried frequency span, followed by O(k) time/source/ambiguity checks.
Track insertion, movement and removal update the index; sorted-array updates
can cost O(n). Closed observations remain indexed through the pairing
retention period, and merges use their current discovery IDs.

### Decode-anchored ADS-B

A verified on-air decode can identify an emission even when no spectrum observation
covers it. The explicit protocol list is currently **ADS-B only**. The normal ambient
confirmation rule still applies: a confirmed ICAO identity from distinct messages,
with the decoder's source and tuning verified as `commanded` or `relay`. One SBS
record, a repeated message, unverified tuning and external-input decoders cannot
create an anchor.

Every accepted confirmation for a protocol on the decode-anchor list goes to one `anchor: "decode"` discovery
per source, protocol and nominal channel (1090 MHz for ADS-B). Further confirmations
refresh that record, including after a restart. A source id change creates a new anchor. Its proof comes from accepted ambient
decodes. It does not imply a measured carrier: bandwidth, airtime and signal statistics
use numeric zero placeholders in the API because those measurements are unavailable
(`anchor: "decode"` identifies them as unmeasured). Decode anchors
never associate or consolidate with spectrum discoveries. Even if a qualifying wideband
observation opens later near 1090 MHz, further ADS-B confirmations refresh only the anchor.
Spectrum observations, including nearby spurs, remain unidentified by ADS-B.

The band label reads **ADS-B · N aircraft**. `aircraftCount` counts distinct confirmed
ICAOs seen in the inclusive last 60 seconds, with a ceiling of 4096 (the ambient
accepted-identity ceiling). The per-ICAO window lives only in memory, with one latest confirmation time per ICAO;
when full, the oldest entries leave first. No aircraft list is persisted in `Observation.decode`.
After restart, the count starts at zero and rebuilds from fresh confirmations. The anchor
has no individual aircraft identity. Count expiry runs even without further confirmations.
Journal entries and discovery events publish count changes or a new 10-second last-seen bucket,
limited to one write per anchor every 5 seconds, with a final latest-state flush on stop.
Removal journals a single delete.
Before rate limiting, writes are at most one per bucket plus one per count change
(confirmations bound increases; expiry bounds decreases). With rate limiting, a busy
minute uses at most 12 rate slots plus creation and final flush, independent of aircraft count.
The persisted anchor stays constant size. Identify at the exact canonical anchor frequency
with explicitly zero bandwidth (the anchor discovery’s unmeasured width) reports
“already decoded: ADS-B, N aircraft” without a trial only while its aircraft count is positive
or its last confirmation is within 60 seconds. Frequency-only requests and measured spectrum
targets run the normal trial. A stale anchor also runs the normal Identify trial and must
receive fresh qualifying spectrum evidence; the anchor itself supplies no measured track.
Without such a track, the trial waits for a signal and ends with no signal at its deadline.
Identify on a 1090 MHz spectrum
observation can receive fresh ADS-B confirmations only when its measured occupied bandwidth
is at least 250 kHz. Width filtering happens before choosing the nearest track, so a closer
spur cannot hide a wide track. Anchor trial memory records the first accepted outcome and
subsequent outcome changes, rather than counting messages as trials. Narrow carriers and
spurs cannot complete Identify as ADS-B.
Spectrum trial success is recorded only after the running Identify trial accepts the
confirmation for its target, epoch and deadline; the spectrum discovery remains
unidentified by ADS-B.
The inclusive window expires at confirmation time + 60,001 ms. There is at most one
timer per anchor, bounded by discovery capacity, cleared on removal and scanner stop.
Reads, snapshots and events share the last published state and revision. Pending count
changes publish within 5 seconds; a refresh that changes neither count nor the 10-second
last-seen bucket stays pending by design until another publication or the final flush.
After a crash, pending confirmations since the last publish are lost: up to 5 seconds
of count changes and lastSeenAt refreshes within the current 10-second bucket.
Observation count and lastSeenAt restore to their last published values; aircraft count
restarts at zero. This is a count of confirmed aircraft,
not every aircraft that produced a single message. Anchors use the same discovery
capacity, inactivity retention, Keep and Ignore rules as spectrum discoveries.

Why only ADS-B: `src/core/spectrum/pipeline.ts` integrates default 50 ms blocks and
has a sliding default 5 ms short path; FFT hop is `fftSize × (1 − overlap)` samples.
An approximately 120 µs ADS-B message occupies only 0.024 of a 5 ms window, about
16.2 dB integration dilution, before its broad pulse energy is spread across bins.
The live failure is therefore consistent with the detector missing these emissions.
AIS's approximately 26 ms burst spans several short windows, and rtl_433's OOK packet
trains can also trigger the short path (which specifically handles bursts up to
15 ms). Their short tracks may fail the channel gate's 100 ms burst / 300 ms normal
minimum, but an accepted ambient decode can already admit a pending observation.
Failing that gate is different from failing to open an observation: there is no
code evidence that all AIS or rtl_433 emissions structurally evade tracking, so
neither is in the anchor list. Sweeps observe only their current hop (default dwell
is settle plus three integration blocks); a missed off-hop transmission supplies
no verified decode and cannot create an anchor.

### Digital voice identity checks

A dsd-fme sync alone is a protocol hint. DMR VC\*/VC1–VC6, IDLE,
unlabelled bursts and R12U never confirm a color-code identity, even when
hundreds of bursts repeat it. They appear as DMR-like digital sync, not decoded,
and remain weak evidence. The most frequent sync-only color code may appear in
the summary as `CC 3 (unverified)`; it is never a `dmr-cc` identity.

DMR needs two matching content-verified occurrences: a clean PI, VLC, TLC,
CSBK, MBCH, DATA or USBD burst, or checked voice link control carrying
both target and source. Pairing is by CC on the same channel across slots:
any checked burst type except voice LC carrying both TG/SRC can confirm at
least 2 seconds and at most 5 minutes apart, even with identical printed content.
These bursts may come from one keying (hang time or VLC + TLC); the spacing is
accepted for identification and does not prove independent transmissions.
Closer repeats count once and retain the first burst's time for the separation
check. Unchecked sync or CC-only hints never confirm. Voice LC with both TG/SRC
still requires distinct link content and uses the latest CC from its own BS
slot, or from the single MS channel, in its current burst sequence. Unknown CC,
sync loss, protocol changes and sequence timeout clear that association. Header and voice-LC
verification waits until the next DMR sync closes the burst (normally one burst
of latency); a non-DMR sync or any continuation CRC/FEC error rejects it.
Stop or silence timeout flushes unfinished headers and voice LC as unverified,
without TG/SRC evidence. A Key-line error also rejects the burst: voice LC can
print before a Key line and the later burst CRC result (`dmr_flco.c:403,778`,
`dmr_dburst.c:627`). Call start/end and talkgroup display still process LC
immediately. CRC/FEC
errors and `-RAS` output do not count. MBCC continuation bursts have no own
CRC and never confirm CC (dsd-fme ed1d1d6 `dmr_dburst.c:115–119,627`);
only the completed block is CRC-checked by `dmr_block.c`. MS prints CC before
slot-type decoding (`dmr_ms.c:676–686`), so header confirmation also requires
the closing DMR sync to print the same CC. Voice LC never borrows a CC from
a still-pending MS header; its checked TG/SRC remain usable with CC unknown.
`Sync: no sync` discards pending
headers, voice LC and CC context but does not split an active call by itself.
Checked voice LC with paired TG/SRC but no known slot CC remains weak
`digital-undecoded` evidence, with summary `DMR voice, TG/SRC verified, CC unknown`
and a `DMR` tag. It cannot become decoded/proven without a CC identity.
Later sync-only hints cannot replace this checked TG/SRC result; newer checked
TG/SRC evidence may replace it.
The interpreter retains at most 256 scoped TG/SRC voice-LC pairs, evicting oldest.
CACH/burst FEC errors
produce no evidence. Near the tuner centre, within the existing DC admission
guard, digital voice additionally needs paired checked voice link control with
both talkgroup and source before it can be decoded.

The same conservative rule applies to other dsd-fme protocols: P25 NAC and NAC/CC
remain unverified even beside a recognized frame type. In dsd-fme,
`dsd_frame.c:34–36` prints cached state, and `390–396` updates NAC/CC only
when BCH corrects the NAC. No supported output binds the printed NAC to the
current checked NID, so P25 identity stays unverified. NXDN accepts current RAN
on CRC-checked
FACCH2/UDCH output, including the exact ` RAN 03  FACCH2` spacing
(ed1d1d6 `nxdn_deperm.c:1390–1437`). CRC failure prints eight spaces instead
of RAN, so it supplies no identity. SACCH/CAC print cached RAN
(`nxdn_deperm.c:466,564,762`) and remain unverified. YSF FICH sync, unchecked
D-STAR MY headers and dPMR's cached channel code do not establish identities.
Unknown output formats default to unverified.

Persisted dsd-fme evidence requires explicit `contentVerified: true` provenance
in snapshots and journal replay before a restored identity can be decoded/proven. Legacy rows without it retain
only digital sync evidence and an unverified DMR color-code hint. Historical
stored discovery identities remain association history so restarting cannot join
previously separate families; they are not decode proof. Published identities and
saved call metadata require checked content: either the discovery’s persisted
verification flag or a retained checked decode proving its current protocol and
identity, including co-channel observations. Observation evidence sets the flag
so verification survives retention. A verified merge survivor adopts another record’s
identity only when that record has checked content for its identity. Merges preserve checked metadata and discard
unchecked call metadata and encryption flags before transferring provenance; identity, search and
encryption filters match the published values. Stored discoveries retain an internal
`contentVerified` flag for checked evidence whose observation was never retained or
has since been trimmed; this flag alone grants no decoded/proven observation proof.
Live evidence and restored evidence use the same normalization, so new snapshots
and journal replay reproduce the live store exactly. Replay uses the
original source epoch's tuner centre retained by the scanner, rather than the
narrow capture's channel centre. The capture index stores source and epoch but
not tuner centre; if that epoch's centre is no longer known (including bounded
history eviction), the DC guard conservatively requires checked voice LC.

### Wireless M-Bus ambient identification

Wireless M-Bus from the live rtl433 decoder uses the existing `ism` scanner
protocol and `ism-model` identity (`Wireless-MBus:<id>`). As for other rtl_433
devices, two distinct CRC-bearing messages must satisfy the existing source,
tuning-trust, timing and unique-observation association rules. Activity rows
use the more specific `wmbus` protocol. The narrow replay captures do not meet
the C/T uplink's 1.2 Msps minimum; identify these meters through live ambient
output. See [decoder setup](DECODER-GUIDE.md#wireless-m-bus-meters-86895-mhz).

Wireless M-Bus meter IDs and readings are personal data, stored like other sensor IDs.
Validation so far uses synthetic fixtures; live meter decodes have not been observed.
